The ball isn't straight ahead — it's off to the side. Your job: send your bot on the shortest straight path to the ball, then kick it at the goal.

You need your Distance Lab equation, the Launch Sequence code, a tape measure, and the ball your teacher places.

1 Measure where the ball is

From your bot's start, measure across (to the side) and up (forward) to the ball. That makes a right triangle.

3 Turn the distance into blocks

Your bot drives in blocks: one block = 27 cm (the runBlockCnt command). Divide your distance by 27 and round to the nearest whole block.

Blocks are the same real distance on every bot, fast or slow — so your geometry stays true no matter your stats.

4 The angle to turn

Before it drives, the bot turns to face the ball. How far does it turn?

The easy case — 45°

If the ball is the same distance across as up (across = up), your triangle is half a square — and a square's diagonal is always 45°. No calculator needed. Set the ball up this way when you can.

The general case — any triangle

If across and up are not equal, the angle is tan⁻¹( across ÷ up ). Use the tan⁻¹ (or atan) key: angle = tan⁻¹( ÷ ) = °

No calculator? Draw the triangle to scale and measure the angle at the bot with a protractor.

One rule, three cases
angle = tan⁻¹(across ÷ up)
across = 0 → 0° (straight shot)  ·  across = up → 45°  ·  otherwise → the tan⁻¹

Missed? Change one thing.

Stopped short or long → your blocks are off. Re-check c ÷ 27.

Ended up left or right of the ball → your angle is off. Re-check tan⁻¹(across ÷ up), and that you turned back the same amount.
