The Launch Sequence

Team: Names:

The ball isn't straight ahead — it's off to the side. Your job: send your bot on the shortest straight path to the ball, then kick it at the goal.

You need your Distance Lab equation, the Launch Sequence code, a tape measure, and the ball your teacher places.

1 Measure where the ball is

From your bot's start, measure across (to the side) and up (forward) to the ball. That makes a right triangle.

Across: cm    Up: cm

2 Find the diagonal — the distance formula

The straight-line distance to the ball is the long side of the triangle. Square the two sides, add them, take the square root:

Use the √ key on a calculator. If the two sides were 60 and 80, the diagonal comes out to a whole number — try it.

3 Turn the diagonal into a drive time

Now use your own equation from the Distance Lab to find how long to drive that distance.

Time = ( diagonal − start-up ) ÷ speed = seconds
In milliseconds: × 1000 = ms
Remember the sign: start-up is negative, so subtracting it adds (diagonal + 7 for the worked bot).

4 Aim, launch, check

6 The Launch-and-Score competition

When your launch reliably reaches the ball, the code kicks it at the goal (the kick strength is your Power²). Now compete: most goals out of 5 launches wins.

Everyone launches from the same spot at the same ball, and everyone drives the exact distance from their own equation — so the winner is the team whose math and aim were sharpest.

b. Your diagonal was cm. Which is shorter?

c. The diagonal is always shorter than going around two sides. That's why a good launch takes the straight path — it reaches the ball faster.
