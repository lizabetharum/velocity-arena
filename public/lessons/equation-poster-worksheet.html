Thursday you teach a visitor standing at your station. This poster is what you teach from. Build it today.

Every number on this poster already exists. It is on your Day 13 Distance Lab sheet. You are not solving anything new. You are copying your work onto something a stranger can read from six feet away.

Start here Put these on the table
  • Your Day 13 Distance Lab sheet, all 11 steps filled in
  • Your stat numbers — Speed, Power, Endurance, Turning (they add to 20)
  • A photo of your team with your bot. Take it now if you don't have one.
  • One device logged into Canva

If your Day 13 sheet is missing the equation in Step 7, finish that first. Nothing else on this poster works without it.

1 Set up the file and get your link

Do these four steps in this exact order. The QR code will not work if you skip ahead.

  1. In Canva, click Create a design → Posters → Poster (18 × 24 in). Portrait, tall way up.
  2. Rename the file at the top of the screen to your team name. Your teacher has to find it later.
  3. Click Share (top right) → set the dropdown to Anyone with the link → set the second dropdown to Can view → click Copy link.
  4. Paste that link somewhere you can get it back — a note, a chat to yourself, anywhere. You need it in 30 seconds.
Why the link comes first

Your poster is empty right now and that is fine. The link works from the moment the file exists and it does not change while you build.

A Canva QR code is frozen the second you make it. You cannot point it somewhere else later. Get the real link now, build the code from it, then design the poster.

2 Build the QR code
  1. Click Apps in the left panel.
  2. Search QR Code. Pick the app called QR Code (the first one).
  3. Paste your link into the URL box.
  4. Click Generate code. It drops onto your poster.
  5. Drag it to the bottom right corner. Make it about 2 inches square.
Make it scannable

Black code, white background. No colors, no photo behind it.

Leave empty space all the way around it. Nothing touching the edges.

Test it before you print. Point a phone camera at the screen. If it does not open your poster in about two seconds, delete the code and build it again from a fresh copied link.

4 Write the equation correctly

Copy your two numbers off Day 13 Step 7 and write them here first, before you type anything into Canva.

distance = × time +
speed (m) · cm per second | start-up cost (b) · cm
Keep the minus sign

Your start-up cost is negative. Day 13 Step 6 told you to keep the minus sign, and it still belongs there.

Do not write + −7. That looks broken. Write it as a subtraction instead:

distance = 22 × time − 7

That example team drives 22 cm every second and loses 7 cm getting up to speed. Your two numbers will be different. The shape stays the same.

On the poster, show both lines

distance = 22 × time − 7

y = 22x − 7

Visitors who know algebra recognize the second line. Visitors who don't understand the first one. Put both up.

5 Get your graph onto the poster

Pick one. Both are fine. Option A is faster and keeps your handwriting on it.

Option A · photograph it

Lay your Day 13 sheet flat. Shoot straight down, not at an angle. Good light, no shadow from your own head. Upload it to Canva and crop to just the graph.

Check: can you count five separate dots in the photo? If not, go over them darker with a pen and shoot again.

Option B · rebuild it in Canva

Click Apps → search Charts → pick a scatter or line chart. Type your five drive times down one column and your five actual distances down the other.

Label the bottom Drive time (seconds) and the side Distance (cm). A graph with no labels tells a visitor nothing.

6 The six-foot test

Stand up. Back away from the screen until you are about six feet from it. That is where a visitor will stand.

  • Can you read the team name?
  • Can you read the equation?
  • Can you count five dots on the graph?
  • Is the QR code black on white with clear space around it?
  • Did the QR code open your poster when you scanned it?
  • Do your four stats add to 20?

Any box you can't check, fix it before you move on. Bigger text is almost always the fix.

7 The wall sign — do this before you leave

Separate from the poster. One sheet of paper, thick marker, taped to the wall. Tomorrow the class lines all of these up and compares them.

TEAM NAME

letters at least 3 inches tall

Two things only. Nothing else goes on this sheet.

  • Team name across the top
  • Your equation underneath, bigger than the team name
  • Letters at least 3 inches tall — about the height of your palm
  • Thick marker. Not pencil. Not ballpoint.
  • Taped at eye level, next to the other teams' signs
Twenty-foot check

Walk to the far wall and turn around. Read your own sign out loud.

Could you read it from back there? YES NO

If no, take it down and write it bigger. This is the whole point of the sign.
