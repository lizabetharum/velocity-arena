Launch Sequence
Student Tuning Guide

Your launch sequence runs once, the moment the match starts. Its whole job is to drive out of your corner and set up the opening kick. This page shows you the default path, the code you can change, and exactly what to tune when it doesn't line up.

01 The one big idea

End your launch with these three things in a straight line:

Your bot → The ball → The goal

Your kick sends the ball the way the bot is facing, so finish the launch behind the ball, pointed straight at the goal — and stop a little short of the ball so the sonar can see it and the code can aim the kick.

02 The path (start corner 0,4)

Field is 8 ft × 4 ft, so (4,2) is dead center — where the ball sits.

LegFrom → ToMoveDistance
1 (0,4) → (0,2) centerline turn LEFT 90°, drive forward 2 ft ≈ 61 cm
2 (0,2) → toward (4,2) center field turn RIGHT 90°, drive forward stop ~40 cm behind the ball

You do not drive all the way to the ball. Stop ~40 cm short — that's the sonar's detect range. The locked code then rolls in the last bit and takes the shot.

03 The code (student zone)
MakeCode · TypeScript
    // Leg 1: turn LEFT toward the centerline, then drive ~2 ft.
    CutebotPro.trolleySteering(CutebotProTurn.LeftInPlace, 90)
    basic.pause(150)
    CutebotPro.runBlockCnt(2)          // 2 blocks × 27 cm = 54 cm
    basic.pause(150)

    // Leg 2: turn RIGHT to face the goal, then drive to ~40 cm behind the ball.
    CutebotPro.trolleySteering(CutebotProTurn.RightInPlace, 90)
    basic.pause(150)
    CutebotPro.runBlockCnt(3)          // 3 blocks × 27 cm = 81 cm
    basic.pause(150)

runBlockCnt(n) drives exactly n × 27 cm — the same real distance on every bot, fast or slow. That's why we use it for the launch.

04 What to tune (and how)
1

Turn direction — check this FIRST.

Left vs. right depends on your start corner and which way the bot faces. This default is built for corner (0,4). Run it and watch Leg 1: if the bot drives into a wall instead of toward the middle, your first turn is backwards — swap LeftInPlace ↔ RightInPlace (flip the other turn too, so they stay opposite).
2

Distances — convert your own measurements. 1 block = 27 cm.

Distance≈ cm≈ blocks
1 ft30.5 cm1.1
2 ft61 cm2.25
3 ft91 cm3.4
4 ft122 cm4.5
Measure how far your start spot is from the centerline (Leg 1) and from the ball (Leg 2), then divide each by 27 to get your block count.
3

Stop short of the ball on purpose.

  • Bot stops and just roams instead of kicking → Leg 2 is too short, add a block.
  • Bot shoves the ball sideways before aiming → Leg 2 is too long, drop a block.
4

The two turns must match.

Both are 90° so you end square to the goal. If your bot consistently finishes angled (wheel slip on turns), tune both turn numbers together — try 88° or 92° until it lines up.
5

Leave the border sensor paused during the move.

Don't turn border detection on mid-launch. You start Leg 2 while still on the back-line tape, and switching it on there makes the bot think it hit the edge — it backs up and spins instead of launching. (This is the "stuck on the black line" bug.) The locked code turns border safety back on after you reach open field.
6

Change one number at a time.

Adjust a single block count or turn angle, run it, watch where it stops, repeat. Changing two things at once makes it impossible to tell what helped.
7

Batteries and floors drift.

A fresh battery drives a hair farther per block than a low one, and slick vs. grippy floors change your turns. Re-check your launch after a battery swap or on a new field.
