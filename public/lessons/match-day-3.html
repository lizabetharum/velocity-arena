The 20% Gap Rule
One new thing today — learn it before Round 1 starts

Engineers usually allow a small gap between prediction and reality because real systems are noisy. In this challenge, 20% is our tolerance band. Under 20% means the model was close enough. Over 20% means one input probably needs attention.

Key Words
Prediction
The number of goals you think you will score — written before the match.
Actual
The number of goals you actually scored — recorded after the match.
Gap
How far off your prediction was. We show it as a percent (%).

The Gap Formula
How to Calculate Gap %
gap = | prediction − actual |
divided by prediction, then × 100
The | | symbols mean "ignore the minus sign" — always a positive number.
Why divide by the prediction?

We divide by the prediction because a 2-goal miss means something different if you predicted 3 goals than if you predicted 10 goals.

Predicted 3, missed by 2
2 ÷ 3 ≈ 67%
Predicted 10, missed by 2
2 ÷ 10 = 20%
Step-by-Step Example
1
Predicted 5 goals. Scored 3 goals.
2
Find the difference: |5 − 3| = 2
3
Divide by your prediction: 2 ÷ 5 = 0.4
4
Multiply by 100: 0.4 × 100 = 40%
!
40% is bigger than 20% → Write the sentence.

When Gap > 20% — Write This Sentence

A variable is one input number in your model that can change, like Speed, time, decay rate, or starting value.

The Sentence Frame

"The variable my model missed was _____ because I used _____ in my prediction, but the match showed _____."

1
Look at the Speed value you plugged into your formula. That's your assumed input.
2
Your actual goals suggest a different effective Speed. Back-calculate: what Speed would have predicted your actual score?
3
Name both numbers. One sentence is enough.
Example sentence
"I used Speed = 6 in my prediction, but based on how many goals we scored, our bot's effective Speed was closer to 4."
