5 Subtract your two runs

Do this arithmetic first. Do not write down what it means yet.

Trial 3 distance − Trial 2 distance = cm
Talk about it before you write

What does that number represent? Your two runs were 2 seconds and 3 seconds. Say your answer out loud to your team first, then write it.

Why subtracting works

Your 2-second run and your 3-second run both started from a standstill, so both lost the same amount of distance getting up to speed.

When you subtract one from the other, that loss cancels out. What is left is one clean second of driving, with no starting up inside it.

So our improved speed estimate is cm per second.
Bigger or smaller than your first guess in Step 1? Why do you think that is?
This subtraction gives cm per second only because your two runs were 1 second apart (2 s and 3 s). If you used two other times, divide by how many seconds apart they were.

6 Find your start-up cost

Your bot spends part of the first second speeding up instead of moving at full speed. The distance it loses doing that becomes a negative starting value in your equation.

Now work out how big that lost distance is:

A · Full-speed distance: improved speed × 2 = cm (what 2 s would be at full speed the whole way)
B · What we actually measured at 2 seconds: cm
Start-up cost = B − A = cm
Do B − A, in that order. Your bot went less far than full speed, so this comes out negative. Keep the minus sign — it belongs in your equation.
