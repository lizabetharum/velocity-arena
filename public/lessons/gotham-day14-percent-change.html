Day 14
Percent Change: How Big Was the Correction?
Put a number on how much fixing your method changed your speed
ENES ← Lessons

Percent change tells you how big a change is compared to where you started. It turns a raw change into a fair "how much bigger (or smaller)" number.

Today: learn it, practice it, then use it on your own bot's speed.

1
Why not just the raw change?

Two changes can be the same size but mean totally different things.

Same +5, very different story
A bot speeding up from 10 → 15 cm/s gained 5 — that's a big deal. A bot going 100 → 105 also gained 5 — but that barely matters. Percent change captures the difference: 10 → 15 is a 50% jump; 100 → 105 is only 5%. Same raw +5, very different percent change.
2
The formula
percent change = new − oldold × 100
⚠ The one trap
You divide by the OLD number — where you started from. That's what the change is measured against.
Worked: 20 → 25
new − old = 25 − 20 = 5
5 ÷ old = 5 ÷ 20 = 0.25
0.25 × 100 = 25%
3
Practice — work through these

Do each on paper first, then type the percent. It checks as you go. Watch the sign — up is positive, down is negative, and you always divide by the old number.

1. A bot's speed went from 40 to 50 cm/s.
percent change = %
2. A bot's speed went from 30 to 36 cm/s.
percent change = %
3. A bot's speed went from 20 to 30 cm/s.
percent change = %
4. A team's 1-second guess was 25 cm/s; done properly it was 40 cm/s.
percent change = %
5. A team's prediction gap shrank from 16 cm to 4 cm. (It went down.)
percent change = %
6. A bot slowed from 50 to 45 cm/s as its battery drained.
percent change = %

A drop (5 and 6) is a negative percent change; a rise is positive. If your answer looks huge or tiny, check that you divided by the old number, not the new one.

4
Same raw gain — who improved more?

Two teams each made their bot 6 cm/s faster. Same raw gain. Find each team's percent change, then read the takeaway.

Team A: 30 → 36 cm/s.
percent change = %
Team B: 60 → 66 cm/s.
percent change = %
The whole point of percent change
Both gained +6, but Team A jumped 20% while Team B moved only 10%. Compared with where they started, Team A improved twice as much. The raw number (+6) hides that; percent change shows it.

Say it on your poster

That sentence is your Goal-1 evidence: a change you made to your model, and how much more accurate it made your speed.

★
Challenge — for fast finishers

Two-step problems. Try these only once you've finished the eight above.

A. Work backwards. A team's speed went up 25% and landed at 50 cm/s. What was the old speed?
old speed = cm/s
B. A percent of a percent. A bot sped up 20%, then sped up another 10% from there. What is the total percent change from the start?
total change = %

A: "up 25%" means the new speed is 1.25 × the old — so you divide, not multiply. B: the second 10% is taken on the bigger number, so the two percents don't simply add to 30% — start from 100 and step through.
