Day 14
Percent Change: How Big Was the Correction?
Put a number on how much fixing your method changed your speed
← Lessons

In the Distance Lab you first guessed your speed from the 1-second run. Then you corrected it using two longer runs. Percent change puts a single number on how much that correction moved your speed.

You'll need two numbers from your Distance Lab sheet: your first-guess speed (Step 1) and your corrected speed (Step 5).

1
Your two speed numbers

Both are cm per second, straight off your Distance Lab sheet.

Example from the Distance Lab: a first guess of 31 cm/s (the 1-second run), corrected to 38 cm/s (from two runs).

2
The formula
percent change = new − oldold × 100
⚠ The one trap
You divide by the FIRST number, not the corrected one. Your first guess is where you started from, so it's the thing you measure the change against.
3
Your result
Fill in both speeds above.
—
percent change from your first guess to your corrected speed
4
Compare with another team

Read another team's two speeds off their poster. Whose method was fooling them more?

What the correction tells you
A big correction means the start-up was really slowing the 1-second run — the naive method was badly off. A small correction means the first guess was already close. Either way, it's why your early predictions missed: you were using the wrong speed.
5
Say it on your poster
"Our 1-second guess was   cm/s. Doing it properly gave   cm/s — a  % correction. That's why our first predictions were off."

That sentence is your Goal-1 evidence: a change you made to your model, and how much more accurate it made your speed.
