From Formula to Equation

Your P(t) formula is already linear — let's write it in slope-intercept form.

The Endurance Decay Formula
P(t) = 100 − (20 − E) × 1.5 × t
P(t) = motor power %  ·  = time in minutes  ·  E = your Endurance stat
🔍 What does each part mean?
P(t) = 100 − (20 − E) × 1.5 × t
P(t)
Power right now
How much power your bot has at any moment in the match. Starts high, goes down.
100
Starting power
Every bot starts at 100% power — no matter what your E value is.
(20 − E) × 1.5
Power lost per minute
Higher E → smaller (20−E) → smaller decay rate. A slope of −27 means your bot loses 27% power every minute. A slope of −6 means it loses only 6% per minute. This is your slope.
t
Time (minutes)
How long the match has been running. Multiply time × decay rate to get total power lost.
🚫 Stop — most students get this wrong

Higher Endurance does NOT mean more decay. The subtraction (20 − E) flips it: a bigger E makes the decay rate smaller.

❌ Wrong: r = E × 1.5
E = 18 → r = 27
High endurance = fastest death?
That's backwards.
✓ Correct: r = (20 − E) × 1.5
E = 18 → r = (20−18)×1.5 = 3
High endurance = slow drain.
Makes sense.

Rule: Higher E → smaller (20−E) → smaller decay rate → longer survival.

Two Times on Your Graph

Your equation has two important moments: when the game locks power at 20%, and where the line mathematically hits zero. You'll calculate both — and they are not the same number.

Look at this graph before reading anything.

One point happens in the game. One point only exists mathematically. Which comes first?

20% Floor Time: Solve P(t) = 20
Set P(t) = 20 and solve for t — when does the game engine lock power?
20 = m · t + 100
−80 = m · t ← subtract 100 from both sides to get the t-term alone
t = −80 / m ← divide both sides by m to isolate t
m is a negative number — dividing −80 by a negative gives a positive result, so t > 0
t = 80 / ((20 − E) × 1.5) ← 20% floor time

You've found the 20% floor time. Now look at your graph — the solid line stops there. The game engine holds it.

But this is a straight line with a slope. Mathematically, it doesn't stop at 20%. If the game rule didn't exist, where would the line end?

At the x-interceptX-interceptWhere a line crosses the horizontal axis. Set y = 0 to find it., the graph touches the horizontal axis. On this graph, that axis is P = 0 — zero power. Setting P(t) = 0 finds that exact point.

True X-Intercept: Solve P(t) = 0
Same process — different target. Set P(t) = 0 instead of 20.
0 = m · t + 100
−100 = m · t ← subtract 100 to get the t-term alone (same move as before)
t = −100 / m ← divide by m to isolate t (negative ÷ negative = positive)
t = 100 / ((20 − E) × 1.5) ← true x-intercept (P = 0)

This number is always larger than your floor time — the game stops the line at 20% before it ever reaches 0.
