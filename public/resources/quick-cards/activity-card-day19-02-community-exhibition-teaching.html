Community Exhibition Teaching Begins
2 of 5 or 6
Day 16 or 19 · Match-Build-Make · 90 min
Follows Expert Setup
MATH: Unit Rate (Motor Power = Speed × 5) | Coordinate Distance (Manhattan) | Proportional Scaling (ratio 5:1)
Before You Start
  • Know all three math concepts: Unit Rate (Motor Power = Speed × 5), Coordinate Distance, Proportional Scaling.
  • Print Visitor Ballots and Challenge Handouts. Station at door or entrance table.
  • Verify all student stations ready: bot, grid visible, scouting report open, Teaching Goal Cards in hand.
  • Place ballot collection box out of student view. Have Visitor Completion Tracker ready.
  • Brief second facilitator if visitor group exceeds 20.
  • *Students teach, you observe.* Intervene only when teaching stalls—imperfect explanations are the work.
Students Produce
  • Lead visitors through three tasks: Physical Bot Handoff, Coordinate Distance, Teaching Goal Card explanation.
  • Demonstrate Motor Power formula using scouting data.
  • Collect visitor ballot feedback on teaching clarity.
  • Write one-sentence exit ticket: hardest moment + what to do differently.
Reference
VISITOR TASKS & SUCCESS: Task 1 — Visitor operates bot ≥ once. Task 2 — Visitor plots two coordinates & states distance. Task 3 — Visitor restates teaching goal in own words. FORMULA CHECK: Motor Power = Speed × 5. Distance = horizontal + vertical (Manhattan).
Timing
0:00Visitor Arrival30-second SAY: explain one job (listen, try, vote). Hand Challenge Handout & ballot. Direct immediately—no standing in middle.
5:00Active ExhibitionObserve teaching quality. Manage visitor flow. Do not coach students in front of visitors.
15:00Monitor EngagementIf a student has no visitors after 15 min, walk a visitor over by name.
45:00Mid-Block CheckPause. Has every student had ≥1 visitor? Is anyone disengaged? Is flow balanced?
75:00Ballot CollectionCollect ballot from each departing visitor. Keep box out of student view.
85:00Exit Ticket & ResetEach student writes one sentence: hardest moment + what to do differently. Collect all cards. Confirm stations ready.
Say This
Open
Welcome. The students at each station will teach you something they built. Do what they ask. Try it. Before you leave, fill out one ballot—who explained most clearly.
Flow Redirect
That station has a wait. Station [X] is open.
Low Engagement
This is [student name]. Ask them to show you their formula.
Task 1 Intervention
Controller first. Explanation after.
Not This
Do not answer math questions yourself. Do not coach students in front of visitors. Do not skip the exit ticket.
Watch For → Redirect
Student explains grid before handing controller.Quietly: 'Controller first. Explanation after.'
Visitor plots one coordinate but can't find the second.'Start at your first dot. Count across, then up. How many total?'
Visitor restates idea without linking same percentage to both quantities.Quietly: 'Ask them: if Speed doubles, does Motor Power double?'
Student misstates formula (e.g., 'Speed times 10').After visitor leaves: 'Motor Power equals Speed times 5. Speed of 7 gives 35. Say that once.'
Student reads report without eye contact or does task for visitor.After visit: 'Try explaining without the paper—you know it.'
Student visibly frozen or distressed with visitor present.Step in briefly. Ask student quietly: 'Tell me one thing about your bot.' Then: 'Tell them that.'
Hard Stop At 85:00: Each student writes one sentence exit ticket on back of Teaching Goal Card. Collect all cards before students leave. Non-optional data for afternoon debrief.
