Day 4 · Context Activation–Game–Debrief · 20 min
MATH: Cognitive load: knowing ≠ accessing under pressure
Before You Start
  • Write down one moment you knew something cold but couldn't access under pressure
  • Prepare monitoring chart with student names
  • Push chairs aside for pair rotation
  • Set visible 90-second timer
  • Have student journals ready for exit reflection
  • *Question sequence in debrief is non-negotiable—reordering collapses the scaffold.*
Students Produce
  • Game scores across three rounds
  • Strategy declaration: fast-risky or slow-careful
  • Exit reflection sentence on Round 3 self-awareness
Reference
STRATEGIES TO WATCH:
• Speed-first blurting: fast, often wrong
• Accuracy-first: pauses 1–2 sec, higher accuracy
• Pattern recognition: names sums without calculating
CHECK: Two things that cost you in this game?
Timing
0:00Context ActivationConnect to yesterday. Activate memory of second-guessing under pressure.
0:02Frame + DemoExplain rules. Demo twice: clean win, then with hesitation. Comprehension check on penalty.
0:05Strategy DeclarationStudents declare approach. Hard stop.
0:05Game Rounds (3×90 sec)Track strategy shifts. Round 1: blurters vs. deliberate. Round 2: shifts. Round 3: rhythm-finders.
0:14Debrief (Q1–Q3)Do not reorder. Q1 behavioral, Q2 quantitative, Q3 conceptual. Name cognitive load only after students demonstrate it.
0:22Exit ReflectionOne sentence on Round 3 self-awareness. Non-optional.
0:23BridgeConnect to Power Stat Challenge. Linear thinking breaks in nonlinear formulas.
Say This
Open
Yesterday you committed to your Turning stat. Who second-guessed their choice at least once? That feeling—knowing something but not being sure when it counts—that's what today is about.
Demo
On three, hold up zero to five fingers, one hand. First to call correct sum gets the point. Wrong answer: no point.
Before Round 1
Tell your partner: fast-risky or slow-careful. This is your comparison point later.
Debrief Core
Q1: Who changed strategy and why? Q2: Did faster mean more points? Q3: Why did speed feel harder?
Not This
Do not name cognitive load first. Do not reorder debrief. Do not skip exit reflection. Vary who you call on.
Watch For → Redirect
Student blurts every round, often wrongAsk: 'You were first a few times but didn't score. What are you noticing?'
Student goes quiet mid-gameAsk: 'One thing to try differently next round?'
Student has found rhythm—pausing, accurate, scoringNote name. Ask: 'What figured out that's working?'
One partner dominates; other disengagesAsk dominating student: 'What's your partner's strategy?'
Student says 'I'm bad at math' instead of naming cognitive loadRedirect: 'You knew it after you hesitated. What happened between knowing and saying it fast?'
Student shows metacognition: feeling → causeName it: 'That's metacognition—thinking about your own thinking.'
Hard Stop Strategy Declaration before Round 1. Exit Reflection (1 min) is non-optional—trim game if needed.
