MATH: Charge Speed % = Power²; ratio & proportional relationships
Before You Start
  • Pre-load MakeCode template; set tape marks per lane (40 cm nose, ball start)
  • Display Power reference table and Efficiency rubric visibly
  • Cut cardboard sheets (~15×20 cm); gather scissors, tape, rulers, measuring tapes
  • Charge Cutebots; verify Bluetooth pairing and worksheet supply (2 pages)
  • Students discovered Power² algebraically. Today: formula meets real hardware.
Students Produce
  • Circled prediction with one justification sentence
  • Assembled cardboard scoop passing all three Scoop Check conditions
  • Recorded averages for Setting A (Power 4) and Setting B (Power 8)
  • Ratio Analysis: charge speed ratio and distance ratio with gap identified
  • Efficiency calculation and Final Decision showing Power²
Reference
**SCOOP CHECK:**
• Hits ball flat (doesn't ride over)
• Strikes center (no sideways deflection)
• Does not block sensor
**FORMULA:**
Charge Speed % = Power²
Power 4 → 16% | Power 8 → 64%
**KEY INSIGHT:**
Charge ratio is exact (algebra). Distance ratio messy (physics). Gap = model limit.

Say This
Open
Formula: Charge Speed % = Power squared. Today you test it on real hardware. Worksheet Page 1, Box 2—make a prediction now.
Launch
Power 4 gives 16%, Power 8 gives 64%. How many times bigger is 64%? [Pause.] Four times. Charge quadruples. Does kick distance also quadruple?
Hard Stop
Circle and sentence on every worksheet before cardboard touches.
Build
20 minutes to build and attach scoop. One rule: hit the ball flat, not sideways, don't block sensor. Go.
Not This
Don't suggest shapes or demonstrate. Teams discover through iteration. If stuck: ask 'Does it hit flat?'
Watch For → Redirect
Angled or narrow scoopsAsk 'Does it hit flat?' and 'Center hit?' No shape suggestions; they iterate during testing.
Subtraction not ratio (64−16=48)Probe: 'How many times bigger is 64 than 16?' vs. 'How much bigger?' Don't move on until clear.
Power confusion (8÷4=2, says 'Power doubled')Point to formula. 'Power 4 gives _%? Power 8 gives _%?' Make math visible.
Code before paper charge speedClose laptop. 'Paper first, every time.' Repeat each setting.
Distance ratio far from 1.5–3 rangeVerify: nose on mark? Ball position consistent? Measurement method consistent. Re-run one.
Redesigning scoop at Min 55 with no data'Use most consistent scoop. 15 min left for both settings. Data is output, not scoop.'
