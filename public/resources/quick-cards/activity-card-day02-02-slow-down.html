Slow Down, Slow Down
2 of 7
Day 2 · Hook-Improv/Physical · 15 min
Follows Failure Bow
MATH: Endurance formula: P(t) = 100 − (20 − Endurance) × 1.5 × t
Before You Start
  • Do the full activity yourself before class. Feel the deceleration cycles and the moment of near-stop.
  • Clear enough floor space for the full group to do jumping jacks safely.
  • Write the Endurance formula somewhere visible for the closing consolidation.
  • Have paper or index cards ready for students to write one-word anchors during exit moment.
  • Plan to hold silence during the single slow jack phase — do not fill it.
  • *The key moment is the absurdity of one very slow jumping jack in near-silence. If you haven't felt it yourself, you can't hold the silence for it. That sensation is the data.*
Students Produce
  • Physical experience of consistent, predictable deceleration (one fewer rep per cycle)
  • Verbal naming of Endurance as the concept behind power decay
  • Identification of the three formula variables: starting speed, rate of slowdown, time
  • One-word anchor written after activity (not 'Endurance' or 'power')
Reference
DEBRIEF QUESTIONS:
1. What would we call it if your bot did this?
2. What exactly changed as you slowed down?
3. Where did you feel it first?
4. What do you need to know to predict power at any point? (starting speed, rate, time)
TALK MOVES: Revoicing, Restating, Press for reasoning, Wait time (7 sec)
Timing
0:00Transition from Failure BowSay: 'Okay. Don't sit down yet.' No explanation. Move immediately into physical sequence.
0:01Full-speed jumping jacksSay: 'Everybody up. Do jumping jacks with me as fast as you can. Full speed. Go.' Model at full speed. Count aloud: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6. Continue 30 seconds.
0:31Slowdown cycle beginsCall: 'Slow down. Five per cycle. 1, 2, 3, 4, 5.' Hold 15 seconds. Progress: 4 per cycle, 3 per cycle, 2 per cycle.
1:45Single slow jack phaseCall: 'Slow down. One. Just one jack. Take your time.' One jack takes 8–10 seconds. Count slowly: '1... and... 2...' Hold silence. Do not move on. Let absurdity land.
3:00Debrief while standingAsk Q1: 'What would we call it if your bot did this?' Pause 7–10 seconds. Wait for 'Endurance.' Then probe: 'Say more. What exactly changed?' Revoice the three variables (start, rate, time).
7:00Closing consolidationSay formula aloud: 'P of t equals 100 minus, open paren, 20 minus Endurance, close paren, times 1.5 times t. You just lived it.' Then exit moment: students write one word for what they felt in last 30 sec.
Say This
Open
Okay. Don't sit down yet.
Open
Everybody up. Do jumping jacks with me as fast as you can. Full speed. Go.
Debrief
What would we call it if your bot did this? [Pause 7–10 seconds. Do not fill silence.]
Closing
Today we felt something that has a name: Endurance. It's not about how strong your bot is at the start — it's about how fast it loses power over time.
Not This
Don't ask debrief questions before students experience the slow jack moment. Don't force the formula connection if it doesn't land — just say 'We're going to put a number to exactly what you felt' and move on.
Watch For → Redirect
Group finishes deceleration fast and energy drops before the slow jack lands.Extend the one-jack phase: 'One more. Really slow this time.'
Some students stop before reaching one rep per cycle.Don't call attention. The partial deceleration experience still lands. Continue.
A student can't do jumping jacks (mobility issue).Quietly before activity: 'You can do whatever movement works for you — arm circles, marching in place. Follow the same pace changes.' Don't draw attention during.
Students make the slow jack theatrical or goofy.Let it go. The theatrical slow jack still delivers somatic experience. Don't redirect unless disrupting others.
Group says 'Endurance' immediately without thinking.Pause before confirming. Ask: 'Say more. What is Endurance actually measuring?' Push past label into mechanism.
Group can't name Endurance after 10 seconds of silence.Try: 'Think about the last 10 seconds. What was happening to your output?' Re-ask. If still stuck, say: 'We're going to give that a name. It's called Endurance.' Continue debrief.
Hard Stop Close with the exit moment: students write one word (not 'Endurance,' not 'power') for what they physically felt in the last 30 seconds. Do not skip this consolidation. This anchor word will reconnect the abstract formula to somatic memory during the Stat Challenge.
