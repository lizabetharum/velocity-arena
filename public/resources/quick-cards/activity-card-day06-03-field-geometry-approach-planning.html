  • Write on board: LAUNCH → HANDOFF → MATCH LOOP
  • Charge robots; have field geometry worksheets at tables
  • Prepare monitoring chart with four strategies

Students Produce
  • Two candidate handoff points using distance formula
  • Step 8 justification with at least two strategic criteria
  • Live robot test observation and result
  • Exit ticket with distance calculation and reasoning
Reference
LAUNCH: Launch sequence → Handoff point → Match loop (automatic, cannot interrupt). BALL ZONE: (4, 2) | GOAL: (8, 2) | Common errors: squaring before subtracting; using x₂=8 instead of 4.
Timing

Say This
Open
Your stats control how your robot moves in the match loop, which is automatic and cannot be redirected. But before the match loop starts, you have a launch sequence. Today: where do you want your robot to be when the launch sequence ends?
Comprehension
Look at the field map for 20 seconds. Find the ball zone at (4, 2), the goal at (8, 2), and the walls. Where your launch sequence ends is where roaming begins. Do NOT write yet.
Distance-min
Does being closer when the match loop starts guarantee your robot stays near the ball?
Not This
Do not treat goal attempts as errors. Do not re-explain arc framing. Do not correct during Explore — confirm and probe.
Watch For → Redirect
Straight-line framing: direction line instead of distanceAsk: 'What does the robot actually do? How does that change which handoff point is better?'
Formula error: squares before subtracting or uses x₂=8Point to worked example. Ask: 'What is x₂ for the ball zone? Recalculate.'
Justification without a numberAsk: 'What was your calculated distance? Return to Step 8.'
Skipped Steps 4–6Ask: 'Which position had lower wall risk? Complete Steps 4–6.'
Robot test with no observationAsk: 'What did you see? Did it hit a wall? Write it down.'
Goal attempt in launch sequenceAsk: 'Where does the match loop start? Calculate that handoff point.'
Hard Stop Before Explore: 20 seconds silent field map viewing. Identify ball zone (4,2), goal (8,2), walls. Before exit ticket: trim Summarize if needed, but never skip exit ticket.
