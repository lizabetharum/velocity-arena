First Bot Drive: Speed as Proportional Relationship
7 of 8
Day 1 · Match-Build-Make · 80 min
MATH: Motor Power % = Speed × 5 (proportional y = kx, k = 5)
Before You Start
  • Pre-pair BBC micro:bits to robots; test USB connectivity
  • Set up tape marks on floor for 2-second bot drives
  • Prepare monitoring chart; print one worksheet per pair
  • Open digital lesson on projector
  • Friction fallacy: intuitive but controlled runs show proportional behavior.
Students Produce
  • Locked prediction: doubling Speed doubles distance?
  • Simplified formula (Speed × 5) on worksheet
  • Three timed bot-drive measurements
  • Claim with data (e.g., 'Doubling Speed doubled distance: 58 cm → 116 cm')
  • Exit Ticket: two calculation problems + reasoning
Reference
LISTEN FOR:
• Formula verification (predict, measure)
• Graph reasoning (straight line through origin)
• Proportional claim with data
MONITOR FOR:
✗ Friction fallacy claim
✗ Wrong simplification (Speed × 100)
✗ Additive language instead of multiplicative

Say This
Open
When driver presses gas harder, what happens? Turn to partner. 20 seconds.
Probe
If driver pressed gas twice as hard, would car go twice as far? Sit with that question—your bot data will answer it.
Hard Stop
Lock a prediction before opening anything. What happens when Speed doubles? Circle it.
Facilitation
You predicted less than double. Your data showed closer to double. Why? Talk to partner. 30 sec.
Not This
Don't say 'friction doesn't matter.' Let the data argue: 'What does your data show about friction?'
Watch For → Redirect
Predicts less than double (friction).Ask: 'Speed 6 = 30%, Speed 12 = 60%. Is that closer to double or less?' Let data override intuition.
Computes Speed × 100 without dividing by 20.Ask: 'Which operation first? Try 100 ÷ 20 alone. What do you get?' Guide to Speed × 5.
Says 'goes up same amount each time' but no proportional language.Ask: 'Divide motor power by Speed each row. What do you get each time?' Help name constant ratio.
Team stalled in digital gates or bot testing.Say: 'One Speed value—use formula. Run one test. Measure. One step at a time.'
Claim without specific data.Ask: 'Show me the numbers. What were your two Speed values and distances?'
Sees line but doesn't explain (0,0) importance.Ask: 'Where does line cross y-axis? What does that mean for Speed = 0?'
