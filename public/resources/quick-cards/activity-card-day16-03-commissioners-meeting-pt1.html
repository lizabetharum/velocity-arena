Final Commissioner's Meeting, Part 1
3 of 4
Day 16 · Match-Build-Make · 90 min
Follows Scouting Report
MATH: Formula output as evidence · Percent change as fairness metric · Systems thinking in rule design
Before You Start
  • Project Team Rankings so students identify reference point
  • Open interactive lesson on all devices before class starts
  • Print or display Formula Reference Cards for all students
  • Set visible timer for 90 min
  • Have Stat Cards and paper/pencils ready for exit check
  • *Formula output, percent change, and systems thinking active. Additive rules preserve gaps; only caps or percentage reductions close them.*
Students Produce
  • Five-section rule proposal with formula-backed evidence
  • Percent change calculation showing gap before and after
  • Mathematical defense against peer objections
  • Recalculated Loadout under winning rule
Reference
FORMULA CORES
1. Formula output as evidence with actual stat values
2. Percent change metric: (new−old)/old×100 shows fairness
3. Systems thinking: every stat connects to formulas
ERROR: Additive shifts don't close gaps. Ask: does gap change when everyone gains the same amount?
Timing
0:00Launch & ContextFrame as Commissioners. Learning goal: construct rule proposal backed by formula evidence. Project lesson URL.
0:05Hard Stop: Reference PointStudents identify team with highest average stat total. Do not start until fairness reference is written.
0:05Tool Work: Sections 1–5Monitor via Teacher Mode. Redirect vague goals, skipped matrices, claims without formulas, blank objection sections. Probe only.
0:30PresentationsEach team: 2 min + 2 min objection window. Teacher revoices, waits 7 sec. Live vote after all present.
1:00Mini-SummaryConnect one moment to percent change. Emphasize: winning proposals had numbers; voted-down ones had opinions.
1:05Loadout AdjustmentRecalculate under winning rule. Ask: What changed? Show formula with new values.
1:25Wrap & Exit CheckMath argument = formula + gap + number. Exit check: one formula calculation under new rule. Non-optional.
Say This
Open
Today you're Commissioners. Identify one unfair stat advantage, propose a rule that fixes it with math, and convince others to vote yes.
Hard Stop
Before Section 1: identify the team with highest average stat total. That's your fairness reference. Write their name before picking your stat.
Tool Work Redirect
Add a formula result with a number. A claim without a number is an opinion, not evidence.
Vote Setup
Talk with your team for 90 seconds. You cannot vote for your own proposal. Everyone adjusts their Loadout under the winning rule.
Not This
Do not write additive shifts. They preserve gaps; only caps or percentage reductions close them.
Watch For → Redirect
Team skips fairness framework, uses vague goalPick the definition your evidence will prove: stat gap or unearned advantage? Which can you measure?
Stat-to-mode matrix skipped before Section 3Which ball mode is affected by this stat? Your evidence must match the mode.
Claim with no formula output or numberAdd a formula result with a number. Show the math.
Objection section blank or minimalName the team with most to lose. Calculate what changes for them. Proposals ignoring objections fail.
Additive shift instead of cap or percentage reductionIf everyone adds 5, does the gap shrink or stay the same? Run the formula and check.
Team says 'nothing changed for us'Calculate percent change: (new − old) / old × 100. Which stat is now your biggest advantage?
Hard Stop Before Tool Work: Students identify the team with highest average stat total and write it down. Fairness reference point must be established. Prevents self-interested, evidence-weak proposals.
