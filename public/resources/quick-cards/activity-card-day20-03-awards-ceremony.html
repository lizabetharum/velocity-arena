Awards Ceremony
Day 16 or 20 · Match-Build-Make Block · 60 min
Follows Post-task
MATH: Linear equations · Percent change · Systems of inequalities · Proportional reasoning
Before You Start
  • Write one specific per-team mathematical moment (when, actual number/concept, what they did with it) — no generic praise
  • Confirm Best Teacher Award ballot result and vote count before students arrive
  • Verify Championship winner from Day 18 and specific match result
  • Prepare per-team moment table with exact wording for each of 6–8 teams
  • Review verbal reflection log format and redirect questions for LO 9.2 assessment
  • *The per-team mathematical moments are the most important facilitation task of the ceremony.* Generic praise does not close the program; specific mathematical recognition does.
Students Produce
  • Verbal reflection on one growth area as a problem solver
  • Specific moment from the program demonstrating that growth
  • Recognition of team's mathematical achievement named in ceremony
  • Contribution to final Free Time block (bot driving, grid work, peer teaching)
Reference
LO 8.2: Communicates math concept clearly for non-expert understanding.
LO 9.2: Identifies growth area + specific moment demonstrating growth.
VERBAL REFLECTION ASSESSMENT:
Y = Met (no redirect)
Redirected = Partial (one redirect, then specific)
N = Not Yet (general only, no specific moment)
Timing
0:00Opening FramingSet up four parts of ceremony. Land on 'Let's start with what I saw.' Do not editorialize program journey.
0:03Per-Team Moments (intro)Say: 'This is not a ranking. This is a record.' Then deliver each team's moment with name, specific number/concept, and math term. Pause 2 sec between teams. Do not put championship winner last.
0:21Championship CelebrationName team and specific result. Frame what it took to get there. Keep under 10 min. Do not add generic consolation language.
0:31Best Teacher Award (LO 8.2)Read ballot evidence. Name one specific teaching moment observed or from visitor feedback. Handle ties: 'Two people earned this.' Do not pause for reaction.
0:38Verbal Reflection (LO 9.2)Call on each student by name (no volunteers). Require both growth area AND specific moment. Use redirects (see key). Record shorthand in log. Do not evaluate responses.
0:56Ceremony CloseThree paragraphs only. Do not extend. Move immediately to Free Time. Resist applause pause.
Say This
Open
Four weeks ago most of you had never driven a robot or written a linear equation from data. Today you already know how to do it.
Per-Team Moment
For each team, I am going to name one moment. One specific thing you did with the math. This is not a ranking. This is a record.
Reflection Prompt
Name one area where you grew as a problem solver. Not as a person — as a problem solver. What do you do differently now? Second: name one specific moment where you saw that growth in action.
Not This
Do not add generic praise to per-team moments ('they were such a great group'). Do not use 'Great answer' or character traits ('worked really hard') as math moments.
Watch For → Redirect
Facilitator adding unprepped generic praise to per-team momentsStop. Return to the moment. The generic praise dilutes specific recognition.
Student not paying attention when their moment is namedSay team name again, wait for eye contact, then deliver moment. Do not skip.
Student reacts emotionally (surprised, dismissive) during moment or awardAcknowledge with a nod and continue. Check in individually after ceremony if needed.
Student names general trait instead of specific moment in verbal reflectionAsk: 'Name the moment where you felt that. Which day or match?' Keep asking until they name a day, match, or calculation.
Student says 'I don't know' or goes silent in reflectionWait 20 seconds. Then: 'Tell me about one match or calculation you still remember. Start there.'
Running behind schedule at student 7 (past 18-min mark)Narrow to specific moment only (not growth area) for remaining students. Capture in log. Do not cut anyone entirely.
Hard Stop Move to Ceremony Close when every student has spoken at least one sentence. Do not extend for quality at the cost of the close. After close, move immediately to Free Time — no pause for applause.
