Know Your Opponent
1 of 4
Day 16 · Hook Block · 20 min
MATH: Comparative gap analysis: threat stat = opponent allocation − own allocation, where gap > 0 AND stat matters
Before You Start
  • Identify which team will use comparative reasoning first
  • Pull one real pair of team allocations from your class and write gap numbers
  • Print/project Championship bracket; pre-sort opponent stat cards by team
  • Prepare bar chart templates and scrap paper for Gut Vote
  • Have Day 15 Prediction Tracker worksheets visible
  • *A stat is only a threat if opponent has more AND it matters in match.*
Students Produce
  • Bar chart comparing own four stats to opponent's (8 bars)
  • Gap measurements for each stat pair
  • Identification and circling of threat stat with justification
  • Exit ticket: threat stat with specific number
Reference
STAT ALLOCATION SCALE: 20 total points. Huge gap = 5–7 points. BAR CHART: Shape and gap matter. THREAT STAT: Opponent > Own AND stat matters.
Timing
0:00ContextPairs recall one opponent and what that bot did well. Hear 2 responses.
0:02Bracket + CheckFind opponent. Task: Compare stats and find biggest threat.
0:04Gut VoteWrite one stat (instinct only). Tally on board. Then check bar chart.
0:05Worked ExampleShow format: if Speed = 6, draw to line 6; opponent = 9, gap = 3. Eight bars total.
0:06ExploreDraw 8 bars, record gaps, circle threat stat. If ahead: write one sentence reasoning.
0:15Summary CheckAt least 2/3 have gaps for all stats AND one circled.
0:16Share DisagreementOne Strategy 1, one Strategy 2 pair explain reasoning. "Who asked the more useful question?"
0:18Gut Vote RevisitHow many changed answers? Name comparative analysis. Relative reasoning matters.
0:19Exit Ticket"My opponent's biggest threat is [stat] because [number]." Scan for vague responses.
Say This
Open
Think back to Season 2. Which opponent did your bot struggle against? What did they do well?
Comprehension
Build a bar chart comparing their stats to yours. Then decide: which ONE stat is the biggest threat? Say: 'I am comparing ___ and finding ___.'
Worked Example
If my Speed is 6, draw to the 6 line. If theirs is 9, draw next to mine to 9. Gap = 3 points. Eight bars total. Format questions?
Hard Stop
Do not draw until you have both stat cards. You need both sets of numbers first.
Not This
Do not pick highest opponent number. Do not pick where you also have high allocation. Do not skip the bar chart.
Watch For → Redirect
Picks highest opponent number without checking own"What is YOUR stat? So what's the gap? If you're both high, does their stat hurt you?"
Picks stat where they also have high allocation"What's YOUR value? Who has the advantage? If you're both strong, who wins on that stat?"
Marks every stat as threat"If everything is a threat, you can't decide. Which ONE gap is biggest?"
Draws opponent bars, forgets own"Your bars are the reference. Draw yours first, then opponent's next to them."
Exit ticket is vagueFlag for Day 17—student hasn't linked reasoning to specific gap number.
Hard Stop Exit ticket is non-optional. Trim Explore time if needed. It is the only individual accountability check. Scan for vague responses—flag for Day 17.
