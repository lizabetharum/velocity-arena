Launch Sequence Plan + Test
4 of 6
Day 6 · Match-Build-Make · 90 min
Follows Field Geometry and Approach Planning
MATH: Hit rate as X out of 3; accuracy vs. reliability; controlled experiments
Before You Start
  • Know which teams need starting position from Field Geometry block.
  • Confirm all robots charged and at stations.
  • Write on board: 'Ball at (4, 2). Launch = your only controlled moment.'
  • Print monitoring chart (page 4) to track team types during circulation.
  • Prepare demo of 'hit ball' vs. 'good kick' — set consistent standard.
Students Produce
  • Two written launch strategies with turn and drive values, plus predictions
  • Trial data table: 6 rows (3 trials per strategy) with Hit Ball and Good Kick
  • Post-kick position record (left wall, right wall, or center)
  • Justification sentence naming hit rate and post-kick position
  • Step 8 controlled experiment: one variable changed, result recorded
Reference
**TEAM TYPES FOR SHARE OUT:**
*Type 1:* Different hit rates (e.g., 3/3 vs. 1/3) — establishes reliability
*Type 2:* Lower hit rate for better post-kick position — Phase 2 dimension
*Type 3:* Step 8 revision worsened performance — valid data
**ACCURACY vs. RELIABILITY:**
Accurate = hits target. Reliable = same every time. Can be one without the other.

Say This
Open
Launch is your only moment of precise control. How do you move to the ball?
Design
Paper first. Two turns, two drives, one prediction per strategy. MakeCode second.
Accuracy
Accurate = hits target. Reliable = same every time. Which row tells you about reliability?
Revision
Change one thing — one turn, one drive time, or one drive distance. Not two. Record if it improved, worsened, or mixed.
Not This
Do not allow unstructured tinkering. Do not re-run for better numbers. Do not skip post-kick position or Data Log.
Watch For → Redirect
Both strategies nearly identical.Redirect before coding. 'These need visibly different paths. What angle puts the robot left or right of (4, 2)?'
Team starting from different position across trials.Confirm starting position before Run 1. If error discovered: note it or ask team to re-run if time allows.
Team gets 0/3 on both strategies.Go to Step 8. 'Change one variable — what do you think is causing the miss?'
Team stuck on justification.'Say your consistency score out loud. Where did the robot end up? Write exactly what you just said.'
Team wants to change two variables in Step 8.'Pick one. Which variable do you think is responsible for the misses?'
Team finishes early and keeps adjusting.'Step 8 is your revision step. Write down what you want to change and do it there.' No tinkering.
Hard Stop Before any robot moves — both strategies must be designed on paper with turn values and predictions filled in. Code does not open until all teams have paper designs complete.
