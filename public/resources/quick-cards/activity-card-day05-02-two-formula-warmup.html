Two-Formula Warm-up
2 of 8
Day 5 · Challenge Block · 8 min
Follows Conditional Simon Says
MATH: Speed = S × (100/20); Endurance = P(t) = 100 − (20−E) × 1.5 × t
Before You Start
  • Write allocation on board: Speed = 7 | Endurance = 5
  • Post formula wall display visibly
  • Print monitoring chart and carry during work
  • *This bridges single-formula retrieval to four-formula synthesis. Q3 has no single right answer—hold the space and name the practice.*
Students Produce
  • Speed output: 7 × (100/20) = 35%
  • Endurance output: P(3) = 100 − (20−5) × 1.5 × 3 = 32.5%
  • One-sentence interpretation
Reference
COMMON ERRORS:
• Inverted Endurance: E × 1.5 × t. Point to wall: "Check parentheses."
• Wrong t value: t=1 instead of t=3. Ask: "How long is the match?"
• Q3 binary thinking: "What in the numbers supports that?"
Timing
0:00SetupWrite Speed = 7 | Endurance = 5. Say: "Today both formulas on same bot, no notes."
0:00:30CheckAsk: "Which two formulas do you need?" Listen for both names; point to wall if needed.
0:01Work (3 min)Say: "Work alone. Pencil. Quiet. Calculate both outputs. Go." Circulate. Watch for errors.
0:04Q1: SpeedAsk: "Motor power?" Target: 35%. If wrong, probe: "What does Speed do with the stat?"
0:05Q2: EnduranceAsk: "Power left after 3 min?" Target: 32.5%. If (20−E) error, point to wall.
0:06Q3: BridgeSay both numbers aloud. Ask: "Does this bot finish strong or fade?" Wait 7 sec. No right answer—hold space. Name it: "That's interpreting outputs in context."
0:07ConsolidationSay: "Speed alone doesn't tell you how a bot performs. It takes both." Hand out Budget Constraint.
Say This
Open
Today you're running both Speed and Endurance on the same bot, same time — no notes. The formulas are on the wall. Retrieval is the point.
Work
Work alone. Pencil. Quiet voice. Three minutes. Calculate both outputs. Go.
Q3
You have two numbers: 35% motor power and 32.5% left at end. Say both aloud to your partner. Is this a bot that finishes strong or fades?
Consolidation
Neither number alone tells you enough. Using both together—that's interpreting outputs in context.
Not This
Do not re-teach formulas. Do not pick a winner on Q3.
