Driving Question + Team Formation
4 of 8
Day 1 · Hook Block · 10 min
Follows Pre-Task Diagnostic: Prior Knowledge Assessment
MATH:
Before You Start
  • One large covered poster with driving question positioned where entire room can read it
  • Teams pre-assigned and recorded; seats arranged in groups of three, tagged by team number
  • Index cards or scrap paper for exit reflection — one per student
  • Do not assign teams on the fly. Improvised team assignment during reveal breaks your presence in the most important 2 minutes of Day 1.
Students Produce
  • Paraphrase of driving question in own words (exit reflection)
  • Introduction exchange: one thing they know, one thing they've never tried
  • Partner restate of what the question is asking
Timing
0:00–0:02Context ActivationAsk: what makes a team actually win, not just get lucky?
0:02–0:04Driving Question RevealUncover poster. Read question slowly. Hold 10 seconds of silence. Partner comprehension check.
0:04–0:09Team FormationCall names at steady pace. Seat teams. Intro task: one thing you know, one thing you've never tried.
0:09–0:10Closing ConsolidationName learning goal. Restate driving question. Preview Day 2. 60-second exit reflection.
Say This
Open
How can you use data, equations, and code to build a robot athlete that wins on math, not luck?
Open
Turn to the person next to you. Don't answer the question. Just tell them: what is this question asking us to think about?
Not This
Do not explain the question further. Do not preview the program. Over-explaining kills the hook.
Not This
Don't editorialise during team formation — no 'you two will work great together.' Just names and team numbers.
Watch For → Redirect
Student lobbies to change teamsTeams are set. You'll understand why later. Don't negotiate or explain.
A student looks isolated during introductionsWalk over. Ask directly: What's one thing you know how to do? Give them a clear entry point.
Energy drops after the revealExpected. The question is big. Don't pump energy back up artificially; let the quiet be productive.
Students ask about robots right awayGood questions. Hold them. We start tomorrow. Do not preview Day 2 content.
Room is chaotic during team formationSlow your pace. Call one name at a time. Wait for that student to be seated before calling next.
Hard Stop Hold silence for ten seconds after driving question reveal. Do not fill it, explain it, or smile reassuringly. If the room is quiet, the question is working.
