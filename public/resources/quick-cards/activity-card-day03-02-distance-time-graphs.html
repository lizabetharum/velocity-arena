Follows Human Robot Programming Challenge
MATH: d = √((x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²) | Per-interval rate of change
Before You Start
  • Solve the graphing task using Card A and Card B; write your explanation.
  • Calculate distance formula for three coordinate pairs.
  • Skim monitoring chart; identify strategies you want to see.
  • *Students read per-interval data, not cumulative. Flat line = consistent speed; declining line = power loss. Most common error: vague explanation without citing specific data.*
Students Produce
  • Distance-time graphs for two runs with one explanation connecting shape to Endurance.
  • Distance formula calculations showing all steps.
  • Navigation Challenge Record sheet with every attempt logged.
  • Worksheet reflection answers on closest attempt and error patterns.
Reference
**Endurance Effect:** Low Endurance → sharp power drop by interval 2–4 → steep downward slope. High Endurance → consistent output → flat line. **Distance Formula:** Pythagorean theorem: horizontal gap |x₂−x₁| and vertical gap |y₂−y₁| form right triangle legs. **Manhattan Distance (Error):** Adding gaps instead of using formula.
Timing
0:00Transition & ContextDebrief Human Robot. Ask what slowing looks like on a graph. Say: 'Let's look at real data.'
0:03Distribute & Read TaskHand out Data Cards. Explain: plot both runs on same graph, write one sentence explaining the difference. Check: 'What goes on y-axis?'
0:08Hard Stop: Label UnitsBefore plotting: 'Write units on x-axis and y-axis. Do not plot until done.'
0:20Explore & Circulate (Part 1)Teams plot runs and write explanation (12 min). Track misconceptions. If vague: 'Which interval shows biggest drop? What does that tell you?' If cumulative: 'Is 50 total or per interval?'
0:32Summarize Part 1Call teams to show graphs. Ask: 'Where do runs separate? Which interval has biggest gap?' Press on explanation: 'Why does low Endurance produce that pattern?'
0:37Walk the Plane: Coordinate FluencyStand at points: (2,3) → (5,3) → (1,4). After each: ask direction and units moved.
0:42Distance Formula IntroductionWrite: d = √((x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²). Silence 60 sec. Then: 'Calculate (1,1) to (4,5). Write every step. Two minutes.' Answer = 5. Run two more pairs.
0:52Connect: Pythagorean LinkBefore sitting: 'This formula is Pythagorean theorem on a grid. Horizontal and vertical gaps are triangle legs. Straight-line distance is the hypotenuse.'
0:57Programming Setup & Navigation WorkSet up MakeCode, UNIT = 27, moveToCoordinate function. Say: 'Record every attempt—coordinate called, reached, distance off. No blank rows.' If random UNIT adjustment: 'Look at last three runs' errors. What pattern?' If undershooting: 'What should UNIT be to cover the distance exactly?'
1:02Debrief & Exit TicketFinish run, record final distance. Worksheet Reflect (2 min). Debrief (5 min): call two teams—which attempt was closest, what changed? Ask: 'Did error get smaller, stay same, or bigger?' Exit Ticket (3 min, non-optional): two independent problems.
Say This
Open
You just spent 25 minutes being a robot. Today you're going to read what your bot actually did, figure out why, and write better instructions. We're starting with the data.
Graph Axes
Before you touch your pencil—tell your team: what goes on the y-axis, and what must your sentence explain?
Units Hard Stop
Write the units on both axes in the corner of your card. Do not plot a single point until both units are written.
Press for Reasoning
You wrote that [quote]. Why does low Endurance produce that pattern? What is Endurance actually controlling?
Not This
Don't say 'wrong.' Instead ask: 'Is 50 the total distance or per interval?' Let students surface and correct the error.
Watch For → Redirect
Plots cumulative (50, 98, 141) instead of per-interval.Probe: 'Is 50 the total distance or per interval?' Surface the error without correcting.
Vague explanation ('Run B was slower') without data reference.Press: 'What caused it to slow? Where would you look to find the answer?'
Calculates Manhattan distance (3+4=7) instead of Pythagorean (5).Ask: 'If you walked 3 blocks east then 4 blocks north, would you be 7 blocks away in a straight line? Draw it.'
Adjusts UNIT randomly; distance-off doesn't improve.Say: 'Look at your last three runs. Error distances are __, __, __. What pattern?'
Error stays inconsistent—suggests turning error, not distance.Probe: 'Is the bot too far away or turned at the wrong angle? How would you test?'
Running but not filling Record sheet.Address immediately: 'Stop. Every row must be filled before the next run.'
Hard Stop Exit Ticket (3 min, non-optional). Two independent problems: (1) Which run has higher Endurance based on graph shape? (2) Calculate distance from (0,0) to (3,4).
