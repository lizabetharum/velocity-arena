MATH: Residual Analysis: actual − predicted; isolate one variable (m, b, or x) to identify bias
Before You Start
  • Pre-populate monitoring charts with team names
  • Pull Match Day 1 and 2 residual records for each team
  • Ensure Day 13 equation worksheets are accessible to Track A
  • Pre-prepare track assignment list
  • Have MakeCode accessible before Track B reaches Step 3
  • *Two residuals same direction = systematic bias. Positive = under-predicted; negative = over-predicted.*
Students Produce
  • Revised equation with one corrected element
  • Hypothesis for Match Day 3 with specific prediction
  • Exit ticket: residual interpretation
  • Reasoning linking test to predicted outcome
Reference
Track A: (1) x estimate error, (2) slope error, (3) intercept error. Track B: allocation must total 20; test trade-off within same envelope.

Say This
Open
Open Lab is 35 minutes. Before anyone opens a data log, I need 60 seconds.
Hard Stop
Tell your partners: did your equation over- or under-predict? Positive residual = under-predicted. Negative = over-predicted. 30 seconds.
Track Routing
Track assignments by name. Track A revises one equation element. Track B tests one configuration. 20 minutes.
Debrief Pivot
Track A changed one element to isolate error. Track B changed one stat to isolate its effect. Same move, parallel process.
Not This
Do not re-route teams. Do not re-derive full equation. Do not allow Track B totals over 20. Do not accept 'I think' as hypothesis without test data.
Watch For → Redirect
Team disputes track assignmentSay: 'Routing is based on Match Day 1 and 2 residuals. You are on Track [A/B]. No re-routing once lab starts.'
Track A re-derives whole equationSay: 'Correct one element. Which—m, b, or x—was furthest from reality?'
Track A blames m and b simultaneouslySay: 'Two data points isolate one cause. Which culprit is clearest? Start there.'
Track B allocation does not total 20Say: 'Does it sum to 20? Which stat shifts to balance? Same total. Test that.'
Track B hypothesis is preference, not predictionSay: 'What did test data show? Why expect that in Match Day 3? Reference one thing from your test.'
Team stuck at diagnosisSay: 'What do you know for certain? Write that. What are you figuring out? What one question could you answer now?'
