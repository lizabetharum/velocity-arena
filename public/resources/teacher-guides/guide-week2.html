Week 2 at a Glance

Day 5 locks everything in. Students submit final allocations. Day 6 introduces field geometry and launches the official season. Days 7 and 8 are match days where the math becomes competitive. The first loss is the most important moment in the program. Your job is to keep it a data event, not an identity event.

Run the 3-step loss-recovery protocol every time. The first loss that lands as "we can learn from this" instead of "we failed" resets how every subsequent loss is processed.

Day 5 · Week 2
Budget Constraint + Dashboard Build
Allocations Locked Today
Session Flow
  • 0–25 minHook: Conditional Simon Says:Warm-up for conditional logic and the full-system equation.
  • 50 minChallenge Block: Budget worksheet — all possible allocations under the constraint. Explore trade-off space. Students draft their allocation with written justification for each stat choice.
  • 20-60 minSnack/Lunch Break
  • 10 minTransition: Dashboard Intro. Simulation Lab. Model a complete gap sentence live: state the formula prediction, state what the bot did, state the gap as a number.
  • 90 minMatch-Build-Make: Simulation Lab. Model a complete gap sentence live: state the formula prediction, state what the bot did, state the gap as a number.
  • 25 minOpen Lab: Teams experiment with the three loadouts they designed in the morning. Each gets tested. The question: which one produces the behavior their formulas predicted?
Milestone: Allocations Locked

After Day 5 Open Lab ends, allocations are frozen. No changes until Season 2 reallocation. This boundary is intentional — it creates the stakes that make Week 3 data analysis meaningful. Hold the line.

Math Focus
S + P + E + T = 20
This is a systems-style constraint. Every unit you assign to one stat is one less for the others. Students experience it as a physical set of choices with consequences.
Key Questions to Ask
  • "If S=8 and E=5 and T=4, what must P equal?"
  • "What happens to your Motor% if you drop Speed by 3 to boost Power?"
  • "Is there a 'right' allocation? What makes one better than another?"
  • "How will you know if your allocation was good — and when will you know?"
Dashboard Setup Check Every team's Dashboard must have: allocation card (all 4 values), formula reference (all 5 formulas), Match Log columns (match #, opponent, result, gap sentence). Verify before Day 6.
Day 6 · Week 2
Field Geometry + Launch Sequence
Season 1 Prep
Session Flow
  • 20 minHook: Equation Relay Race. Teams line up at the whiteboard. First student solves step 1 of a multi-step equation, tags the next student for step 2, and so on. First team to the correct final answer wins. Primes procedural fluency before the interleaved problem set.
  • 50 minChallenge Block: Stat Allocation. Distances between key field positions. How far does the bot travel from start to scoring zone?
  • 20-60 minSnack Break
  • 60 minChallenge Block: Field Geometry + Approach Planning. Distances between key field positions. How far does the bot travel from start to scoring zone?
  • 60 minMatch-Build-Make: Launch Sequence Programming. Teams program the launch sequence as a routine separate from the stat loadout coded in the morning.
  • 15 minMatch-Build-Make: Launch Showcase. Commit to your final sequence and write down your run order. Each team runs their launch sequence once in front of the room. While another team runs, write one specific observation in your dashboard: not 'they were fast' but 'their bot reached the ball in under 3 seconds, which suggests their Speed allocation is above 8.'
  • 10 minOpen Lab: 10 minutes for one final adjustment. Then sequences lock.
Preview first match: Day 7 is the first real match. Teams who haven't practiced their launch sequence will be disorganized on match day. Use the last 15 minutes of Match-Build-Make to run a timed practice launch. Set expectations now.
Math Focus
d = √[(x₂−x₁)² + (y₂−y₁)²]
Distance formula introduced in field context. How far does the bot need to travel? Which path is shorter? Students apply distance to route planning — connecting geometry to their bot's Speed and Endurance values.
Key Questions to Ask
  • "From start position to scoring zone. How far is that? How long does it take at your Motor%?"
  • "Which corner requires the tightest turn? What T value do you need?"
  • "If your bot has Motor% = 40%, how many seconds to cross the field?"
  • "What does your Endurance formula predict for power remaining at the end of a 2-minute match?"
Day 7 · Week 2
Season 1 Match Day 1 + Loss Recovery
First Real Match
Session Flow
  • 20 minHook: Trashketball: Warm-Up Round. Teams solve one equation from the allocation sheet. Correct answer earns a shot. Crumpled paper into a bin. Making the shot is a bonus point. Fast, physical, competitive. Primes competitive energy before the first real matches.
  • 50 minChallenge Block:Endurance Decay Equations in Slope-Intercept Form. Teams use their Endurance allocation to write the bot's power decay equation as P(t) = 100 - rt and calculate r. When r is revealed, name it explicitly: the slope of a line is its rate of change, and r is how fast the bot loses power per minute. Teams find the x-intercept, the minute at which bot power reaches zero. Equation and x-intercept go in the match data log before the first match. This is the first formal slope-intercept form in the program..
  • 20-60 minSnack/Lunch Break
  • 15 minTransition: Introduction to Dashboard. Introduce Dashboard and matchrules. Explain match rules in under 10 minutes: each match is 3 minutes, two teams on a flat field, first team to push the ball across the opposing end line scores a goal. Bots are pre-programmed. After each match, both teams record their score in the data dashboard immediately.
  • 90 minMatch-Build-Make: Season 1 Match 1. Run all matches. Analyst logs results in real time. Post-match: every team writes a gap sentence before leaving the match area.
  • 30 minOpen Lab: Post the physical leaderboard on the wall (or update the Velocity Arena Dashboard Leaderboard). Include the Best Prediction column, which rewards mathematical accuracy, not just winning. Then 20 minutes of open bot time for dashboard review, strategy, or code adjustments. Leaderboard is a permanent artifact from today forward.

3-Step Loss Recovery Protocol — Use Every Time

  1. Acknowledge the emotion. Give 30 seconds of silence. Do not explain or redirect.
  2. Open the Match Log. Find one decision — just one — that connects to the result.
  3. "What would you test differently? Complete: If we change ___, we predict ___."
Math Focus
"We predicted ___, actual was ___, gap = ___"
The gap sentence is the analytical tool of the match day. Every team must write one before their next match. This is where all 4 formulas from Week 1 become a measurement framework rather than calculation exercises.
Match Log — Required Fields
  • Match number and opponent team name
  • Result (Win / Loss / Tie) + score if applicable
  • Prediction made before the match (with formula reference)
  • Actual result observed
  • Gap sentence — all 3 parts present
  • One revision hypothesis for next match
Analyst role watch: The Analyst must log during the match, not after. If they say "I'll remember it," intervene immediately. The Match Log is a real-time document — memory is not a substitute.
Day 8 · Week 2
Linear Equations + Match Day 2 + Rivalry Cards
Rivalry Cards Issued
Session Flow
  • 20 minHook: Math Scavenger Hunt: Leaderboard Edition. Post problem cards around the room, each referencing a real stat from yesterday's leaderboard ('Team A: Speed = 5, scored 3 goals'). Students walk to each card, answer the question on the back (calculate a percentage, write a ratio), and find the matching answer on the next card. Self-correcting chain loop -- if students cannot find the next card, their previous answer is wrong.
  • 50 minChallenge Block: Part 1 (20 min): each team adds a data point to a shared class scatter plot. Speed on x-axis, Goals Scored on y-axis. Teams match card sets: verbal situations, data tables, and graphed lines. They must match three that belong together and explain why.
    Part 2 (20 min): teams use two points from the scatter plot to write a linear equation, slope labeled, y-intercept solved. Every team's equation is different. Share aloud.
  • 20-60 minSnack/Lunch Break
  • 80 minMatch-Build-Make: Season 1 Match Day 2. Same match-day protocol: public prediction, Analyst logs in real time, gap sentence before moving on. Rivalry Cards issued after matches.
  • 20 minMatch-Build-Make:Rilvaly Cards. Each team writes one trash-talk card directed at a specific rival. The only rule: the trash talk must cite a specific stat or data point from the dashboard ('Your Endurance = 3 means your bot slows at minute 4'). No data, no card. Cards go on the wall as persistent artifacts. Adversarial framing motivates deeper dashboard engagement.
  • 25 minOpen Lab: review the scatter plot equation against today's match results.
    Facilitator Note: Prepare Formula Race Cards
Math Focus
y = mx + b
Applied to bot performance: y = predicted win margin, x = a chosen stat value, m = rate of change in wins per unit stat, b = base performance. Students work with real match data to estimate slope — connecting abstract algebra to their own results.
Rivalry Card Format
Rivalry Card — Required Contents
  • Rival team name and bot name
  • Rival's known allocation (from observation or Dashboard, if shared)
  • One stat where your team has an advantage — with formula support
  • One stat where your team is at a disadvantage — with formula support
  • Prediction for head-to-head match result — with reasoning
  • One tactic your team will use in the next match vs this rival
Watch for rivalry turned toxic: Rivalry Cards are analytical tools, not trash talk. If teams start making personal statements about rivals instead of data statements, redirect immediately. "What does the formula say about their allocation?" not "they're just better."
Match Log — Complete Field Reference
FieldWhat to RecordCommon ErrorStandard
Match # REQUIRED Sequential number (1, 2, 3…) for the season Resetting to 1 each day Numbers are cumulative across the season, not per-day
Opponent REQUIRED Bot name AND team name of opponent Writing only "Team 3" without the bot name Always use the bot name — it's the unit of analysis
Result REQUIRED Win / Loss / Tie + score (e.g., W 3–1) Just writing "Win" without the score Score is required — it's the quantified data point
Prediction REQUIRED What did you predict before the match, with formula basis? Filling this in AFTER seeing the result Must be recorded before the match starts. Retroactive predictions are invalid.
Gap Sentence REQUIRED All 3 parts: predicted value / actual result / computed gap Missing the computed gap (3rd part) "We predicted X, result was Y, gap was Z." Z must be a number.
Revision Hypothesis "If we change ___, we predict ___." — one specific revision Writing "we need to do better" (no action) Must name a specific stat change and a predicted effect
Week 2 Critical Risks

First Loss Identity Threat (Day 7)

The first match loss is the highest-stakes facilitation moment in the program. Some students will interpret a bot loss as a personal failure. The 3-step protocol must happen within 5 minutes of the loss. Do not let the emotion calcify.

Allocation Regret After Lock (Day 5)

Teams who lose Day 7's match will immediately want to change their allocation. The lock is non-negotiable. Redirect: "Your allocation is your hypothesis. What does the data tell you about it? Season 2 is coming."

Analyst Not Logging in Real Time

Match Log filled from memory after the match is reconstruction, not data. Retroactive predictions are the most common integrity violation in the program. Check Analysts during the match, not after.

Rivalry Cards Used for Trash Talk

Rivalry Cards work analytically only when the content is data-based. If teams are writing "they got lucky" instead of "their Turning Ratio of 0.50 gives them better corner navigation," the card is off-task. Redirect to formulas.
