Challenge 1Unit Rate

Each robot stat controls one part of the bot. What does one extra point actually give you?

How the formula works

Students set a Speed stat from 0 to 10. The bot's Motor Power is calculated using:

Motor Power = Speed × 5

Worked example: Speed = 4 → Motor Power = 4 × 5 = 20

Unit rate: 5 Motor Power per Speed point. Every time Speed goes up by 1, the bot gains exactly 5 Motor Power.

How coordinates work

Challenge 3Proportional Scaling

When a stat doubles, does the result double? Not always — but with Motor Power, it does. Let's test it.

What is a proportional relationship?

Two quantities are proportional when their ratio stays constant — doubling one always doubles the other.

Motor Power = Speed × 5

Speed 2 → Power 10  ·  Speed 4 → Power 20  ·  Speed 6 → Power 30.
Speed doubled (2→4), Power doubled (10→20). Speed tripled (2→6), Power tripled (10→30).

The big idea

Motor Power is proportional because it multiplies by a constant (Speed × 5). The Charge Speed formula uses Power² — that one is not proportional. Ask a student to show you how those two formulas make very different graphs.

At your station · if GORun the Demo

About a minute per visitor group. The point is the math running on the robot — not winning.

What you say + do

1. Set up. Bot in the corner, ball on its spot, goal at the end.

2. Explain first (10 sec): "Our bot drives two legs — corner to the midline, then straight at the ball — using drive times we got from our distance equation. Then it kicks."

3. Press A. The bot launches, rolls up to the ball, and kicks.

4. Ask the visitor: "Where do you think the drive times came from?" — then show them: ms = (distance − start-up) ÷ speed × 1000.

Offer this"Want to see it miss on purpose? We'll change one drive time and run it again." — change ms2, re-run, and the ball lands short or long. That's the model in action.
Keep it a demo

No score, no bracket, no winner — consistent with today's closing circle. If a kick misses during a demo, say what you'd re-tune and move on. Naming the fix out loud is honest engineering, and visitors respect it more than a lucky goal.
