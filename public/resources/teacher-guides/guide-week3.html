Week 3 — Know Your Data

Days 9–12 · Season 1 Final · Distance Formula · Commissioner's Meeting
Season 1 Final Day 10 Cold Recall Check Day 10 Underdog Reveal Day 11 Commissioner's Meeting Day 12
Week 3 at a Glance

Week 3 is the data-analysis phase. Teams start examining what their allocation decisions have produced. Day 9 runs a third match and a Formula Relay. Day 10 closes Season 1 and includes the Cold Recall Check. Day 11 introduces systems of inequalities and the underdog mechanic. Day 12 is the Commissioner's Meeting, the most complicated day to facilitate.

The most important thing you do in Week 3: Stay out of Commissioner proposals, but make sure it runs. Teams may produce weak proposals. Let them. The class debate is the learning. Your role is facilitating the format, not improving the content.
Day by Day — Week 3
Day 9 · Week 3
Season 1 Match Day 3 + Formula Relay
3rd Match + Relay Challenge
Session Flow
  • 25 minHook: Rivalry Card Response. Each team reads the rivalry card they received and opens their dashboard to verify the cited numbers. If the trash talk is factually wrong, the team marks it 'check your data' with a sticky note and the correct number. If accurate, they make a plan. Cross-team data verification without you mediating.
  • 50 minChallenge Block: Formula Relay — competitive team challenge applying all 5 formulas under time pressure. Teams rotate through 5 stations, one formula each. Results contribute to a leaderboard.
  • 20-60 minSnack Break
  • 90 minMatch-Build-Make: Season 1 Match Day 3. Standard match protocol — public prediction, Analyst logs real time, gap sentence before next match, 3-step loss recovery if needed.
  • 25 minOpen Lab: Teams look at their three match results, spot patterns, and write one hypothesis entry in the dashboard -- 'If we change [stat] from [X] to [Y], we expect [specific outcome] because [cited data].' Track B (Bot Lab): teams experiment freely with the bot. Any useful finding goes in journals before they leave.
Formula Relay design note: The Relay is competitive but individual formula accuracy is the metric. Teams should not be able to skip hard stations. Assign one formula per station — Speed, Endurance, Turning, Power, Budget — and rotate teams through all five.
Math Focus
All 5 Formulas Under Time Pressure
The Formula Relay tests retention without the bot scaffolding. Students who can only apply formulas when the bot is in front of them haven't internalized the math. The Relay exposes this gap cleanly — use it diagnostically, not punitively.
Key Questions to Ask
  • "Looking at the standings — what pattern do you notice between Speed and win count?"
  • "Which formula was hardest in the Relay? Why?"
  • "Does your current standing match what your formulas predicted?"
  • "What one change would you make to your allocation if you could? Why — with a number."
Day 10 · Week 3
Season 1 Final + Distance Formula + Cold Recall Check
Season 1 Closes
Session Flow
  • 20 minHook: Best Match Gallery Walk. Each team posts the stat configuration and result of their single best Season 1 match on an index card. Students walk the room and read every card. Dot-sticker vote: which team had the most mathematically interesting finding -- not who won the most, but who learned the most from one match result. Emphasize that the vote is about learning, not record.
  • 50 minChallenge Block: Distance formula deep-dive. Three positions are fixed for the whole class: A = (4,2) tip-off, G = (8,2) goal, B = each team's own Season 1 ball-landing estimate. Every team runs the same formula but gets different numbers because B is team-specific -- that variation is the point.
  • 20-60 minSnack/Lunch Break
  • 10 minChallenge: Cold Recall Check. Five questions, 10 minutes, individual work, no grade, no notes or devices. Non-bot context. Questions: (1) percent change in motor power between Speed = 6 and Speed = 9; (2) slope-intercept form from slope 2 through (1,3); (3) distance from (2,5) to (7,17); (4) when does P(t) = 100 - 12t reach zero; (5) a bot travels 150 cm in 5 seconds -- speed in cm/sec and distance at that rate for 8 seconds. Diagnostic, not assessment.
  • 90 minMatch-Build-Make: Season 1 Final. All remaining matches completed. Official Season 1 standings locked. Identify last-place team (confidential until Day 11).
  • 20 minOpen Lab: Season 2 Planning. Teams teams look at Season 1 data and name one stat allocation change for Season 2 as a hypothesis with data justification.

Individual, no collaboration, non-bot context means questions are presented as pure algebra — no arena framing. This tests transfer, not recall with scaffolding. Do not return results publicly. Use results to identify students who need formulas revisited in Week 4.

Math Focus

Apply distance formula to actual arena positions. Which team's bot traveled the most efficient path? Compare theoretical (straight line) to actual (observed turns). What does the gap between them represent?
Key Questions to Ask
  • "From the final standings — which allocation style performed best? What pattern explains it?"
  • "Your bot traveled from position A to B to C. What was the total distance?"
  • "What's the difference between the shortest path and the path your bot actually took?"
  • "If you could reallocate for Season 2, what would you change and why — with two data points?"
Last-place team identification: Identify the last-place team after Season 1 Final. Do NOT announce it yet — the reveal happens Day 11 with the underdog mechanic explanation. Treat this information as confidential until then.
Day 11 · Week 3
Systems of Inequalities + Underdog Reveal + Season 2 Reallocation
Underdog Mechanic Revealed
Session Flow
  • 20 minHook: Human Number Line: Stat Rankings.Lay a tape line across the room, labeled 'least important' to 'most important' for winning Season 1 matches. Students stand at the position matching their belief about which stat matters most.
  • 45 minChallenge Block: Proportional Scaling: Scaling Your Stats + Commissioner Proposal Prep. Phase 1 (20 min): Teams calculate what happens when a stat is scaled by 50%. Speed is proportional. Power is not (charge speed = Power², so 50% more Power gives more than 50% more charge speed -- quadratic).
    Phase 2 (25 min): Teams draft a rule change proposal using those scaling calculations as evidence, backed by at least two dashboard numbers and addressing how the current last-place team is affected. This sets up tomorrow's formal Commissioner's Meeting.
  • 20-60 minSnack/Lunch Break
  • 30 minMatch-Build-Make: Underdog Mechanic Announced + Season 2 Stat Reallocation. Announce the last-place team and explain the underdog mechanic (Budget = 22 for Season 2). Teams begin Season 2 reallocation planning using systems of inequalities framework.
  • 65 minMatch-Build-Make: Practice Matches: Test New Loadout Hypotheses. No formal scoring. Teams program and test adjusted loadout ideas and record observations as 'hypothesis' entries, clearly labeled so they can later compare to Season 2 actuals. Low-stakes iteration before the Commissioner's Meeting locks in new rules..
  • 40 minOpen Lab: Teams verify every number in the draft proposal against the actual dashboard. Two check questions: are the cited numbers accurate, and does the rule change genuinely help the last-place team (or does it just help the team proposing it)? Revision before tomorrow's vote.
The Underdog Mechanic
The last-place team after Season 1 gets Budget = 22 for Season 2. All other teams keep Budget = 20. This 2-point advantage is intended to create a credible competitive threat, not guarantee a win. The underdog team must use the extra 2 points wisely, which means applying the systems of inequalities framework to find the optimal allocation for the expanded budget. Do not explain the mechanic before Day 11. The reveal moment is intentional.
Math Focus
S + P + E + T ≤ 20  &&  S ≥ 4  &&  E ≥ 3
Systems of inequalities in the allocation context. The budget constraint becomes an inequality when there's a minimum performance floor on each stat. Students find feasible allocations that satisfy all constraints simultaneously — directly applicable to Season 2 planning.
Key Questions to Ask
  • "What constraint would prevent your bot from being viable? Write it as an inequality."
  • "If you require Motor% ≥ 40%, what constraint does that put on S?"
  • "For the underdog team: how would you allocate the extra 2 points? Which formula justifies that choice?"
  • "Draw the feasible region for your Season 2 allocation constraints."
Day 12 · Week 3
Commissioner's Meeting + Vote
This day is complicated
Session Flow
  • 25 minHook: Lobby the Commissioners. Teams spend 10 minutes circulating in informal conversation. 'Here is our proposal. Here is the number behind it. What do you think?' Then 15 minutes back at seats to finalize based on feedback. Builds peer buy-in before the formal vote.
  • 35 minChallenge Block: Final Proposal Verification. Teams check every number in their proposal against the actual dashboard. Two questions: is the data accurate, and does the rule change genuinely help the last-place team or only the proposing team?
  • 20-60 minSnack/Lunch Break
  • 90 minMatch-Build-Make: Commissioner's Meeting: Formal Presentations + Vote. Each team has 3 minutes to cite two data points, explain reasoning, and describe how the change affects the last-place team. The league votes. Passing proposals become rules. Write them on the wall. After the meeting, teams adjust their Season 2 allocation under the new constraints. After every presentation, ask the room: 'Does this proposal use the data correctly? Can anyone verify the math?'
  • 50 minOpen Lab: Update bots with Season 2 allocations. Test run. Prepare for Season 2 matches starting Day 13. Math Connection closing ritual.
Highest-risk facilitation moment: DON'T coach proposals. Teams that produce weak proposals must present them anyway. The class debate is the learning event. Your job is to ensure the format requirements are met, facilitate the discussion, and run a fair vote. Any facilitator move that improves proposal quality before the vote steals the learning.
Commissioner Proposal Format
Every Proposal Must Include
  • A specific proposed rule change for Season 2
  • Two specific data points from Season 1 match logs supporting the proposal
  • An explicit explanation of how this change affects the last-place team
  • At least one named math concept (formula, inequality, equation) the proposal involves

If any element is missing, the proposal can still be presented — the class will notice. Do not reject proposals for incompleteness. Let the floor do that.

Key Questions to Ask During Presentations
  • "Which two data points are you citing? Where do they appear in your Match Log?"
  • "How does this change affect the last-place team specifically?"
  • "What math concept does this proposal involve? Can you name it?"
  • "Does this change make Season 2 more or less competitive? Why?"
Systems of Inequalities — Allocation Application
Constraint TypeInequality FormBot Context Example
Budget constraint S + P + E + T = 20 Total stats must sum to exactly 20 (Season 1) or 22 (underdog, Season 2)
Minimum speed floor Motor% ≥ 40% → S ≥ 8 Team requires at least 40% motor power to reach scoring zone in time
Endurance floor P(2) ≥ 70% → E ≥ 11.3 Bot must retain 70% power at 2 minutes to complete the match viably
Power ceiling P ≤ 7 Team caps Power to free budget for Speed and Endurance
Combined feasibility S ≥ 8, E ≥ 6, T ≥ 3, P ≤ 7, sum = 20 All four constraints must hold simultaneously — intersection of all feasible regions
Week 3 Critical Risks

Commissioner's Meeting Over-Scaffolding (Day 12)

This is the highest-risk facilitation day. The urge to strengthen weak proposals destroys the authentic tension the lesson requires. If you prep teams too much, the meeting becomes theater. Let weak proposals fail on the floor.

Cold Recall Check Anxiety (Day 10)

The Cold Recall Check looks like a test and feels like a test. Frame it explicitly as diagnostic data for the facilitator, not a grade for the student. Do not discuss results publicly. Use them to target Week 4 support.

Underdog Resentment (Day 11)

Other teams may resent the last-place team getting Budget = 22. Channel this into mathematical analysis: "How would you defeat a team with 2 extra points? What allocation would counter it?" Turn the resentment into a strategy problem.

Formula Relay Competitiveness Displacement (Day 9)

Some teams will focus on beating other teams in the Relay rather than learning the formulas. Reorient: the Relay score is information about formula fluency, not about who is smartest. What does your Relay result tell you to review before Week 4?
