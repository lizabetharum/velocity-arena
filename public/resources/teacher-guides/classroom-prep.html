CHECKLISTClassroom & materials prep

What to set up, print, and stage.

You can print most of these worksheets before the pilot starts. The list below covers every print job across all 16 days — pre-print the whole stack in one pass before Day 1, sort into 16 labelled folders, and you'll never touch the copier mid-pilot. Worksheets go to students and teams; answer keys go only to facilitators. Print this page out too, and check items off as you complete them.

01 Before Day 1 — one-time setup. ~3 hrs · 1–2 days before camp

These are the four things that have to happen before students touch a bot. Do them 1–2 days before Day 1, not the morning of — the bot-training step alone takes time you won't have on opening day.

Insert batteries into the bots

One battery per bot — same direction every time, label side up. Charge the batteries the night before so you're not waiting on partial charges as students arrive.

Train every bot to recognize its lines

Each bot needs its line-following sensors calibrated to the field tape and its compass tilt-calibrated. Plan ~5 minutes per bot — for 12 bots, that's an hour. See the line-training notes in the Match Code lesson.

Charge every device

Laptops or tablets for every team, fully charged. The MakeCode editor runs in-browser — you don't need anything installed beyond a working USB connection.

Create teams of 3

Each team has three roles: Coder, Fabricator, Analyst. Roles rotate weekly from Week 2 on, so every student holds every role by Week 4. Decide the team rosters before Day 1 — not in the room — so the Day 1 launch isn't competing with cliques and conversation. Post the rosters on the wall before students arrive (alongside the Driving Question, Bot Names, and Leaderboard spots) so students can find their team without asking, and so role rotations in Week 2 land on something visible to everyone.

Tip Bot batteries are the single biggest source of "why isn't this working?" frustration. Plug everything in to charge the night before Day 1 and you'll skip the most common opening-day failure mode.
Strategy · the most important move on this page Print every worksheet for all 16 days in one pass, before the pilot starts. All four weeks of materials are listed below. None of them change cohort-to-cohort. Pre-print the whole stack, sort into 16 labelled folders or trays (Day 1, Day 2, … Day 16), keep the answer keys in a separate facilitator binder, and you'll never lose class time to a paper jam. The biggest gift you can give Day 12 is making sure none of Day 12 is at the copier.

One exception: the Math Scavenger Hunt cards on Day 7 are built that evening for Day 8, because each card pulls from the live Dashboard data from Day 7's matches. Everything else can be ready before students arrive.

06 Paper math — how much you'll actually need.

A quick reference for ordering paper or scheduling time at the copier. These are rough estimates for a class of 12 students = 4 teams of 3, the program's reference cohort size. Scale linearly with your class.

For a 12-student cohort ~280 single-sided student copies across all 16 days (every-student worksheets + stat cards + recall checks + diagnostics).
~95 single-sided team copies (4 teams × the per-team worksheets that show up Days 3 onward).
~35 facilitator copies (one of each answer key, kept in your binder).
Plus visitor materials on Day 16 — sized to your guest list, not the cohort.
One pass, before the pilot — that's the whole strategy The materials don't change cohort-to-cohort. Sit at the copier once, before Day 1, and run the entire 16-day stack. Sort each day's pile into a labelled folder. The only exception is the Day 16 visitor materials — those wait for the RSVP list. Everything else can be ready before students walk in.
