How to use this guide

Each activity shows three levels of student work — Approaching, Meets, and Exceeds. The right column gives you the teacher look-for: the specific thinking or calculation that distinguishes each level.

Week-end Benchmark Snapshots summarize what a typical student should be able to do at the close of each week. Use these to calibrate your sense of the class before the next week begins.

These are artificial examples — not transcribed from real students. They are intentionally imperfect at the lower levels. A student at the Approaching level is not a bad student; they are a student in the middle of learning.

Guide

How to Read These Exemplars

Approaching Student attempts the task but makes a procedural, conceptual, or reasoning error. The error is specific and addressable — not a random guess. This is the most common state for students encountering a new concept.
Meets Student applies the formula or concept correctly and shows work. The calculation is accurate. The explanation connects the number to bot behavior in a way that is correct, though often brief or literal.
Exceeds Student goes beyond correct calculation: they notice a pattern, make a strategic connection, anticipate a consequence, or explain the trade-off. The math is correct and the thinking is transferable.

The Teacher Look-For column (italicized, right) names the specific thinking pattern to listen for. It is written as a brief description of what distinguishes that level — useful during circulating, during discussion, and when reviewing written work.

These exemplars cover the primary written activities — the ones where students produce work you can read and respond to. Hands-on match events and Open Lab time are not included; use the Loss-Recovery Protocol and Leaderboard Observation instead.

Week 1 · Pre-Season

Stat Boot Camp (Days 1–4)

Students are building intuition for each stat formula before they are connected to match outcomes. The goal is calculation fluency and the first link between formula output and physical behavior.

Exceeds Student writes: "P(t) = 100 − 24t.
