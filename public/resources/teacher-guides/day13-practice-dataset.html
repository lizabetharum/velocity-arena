Velocity Arena · Day 13 fallback

Build the Equation without real match data

The Day 13 activity needs each bot's stat and win % to build a trend line. If matches haven't run, there's nothing to plug in. This sheet gives the class a ready-made Season 1 — eight bots and four match days — so the lesson runs exactly as written. The math is identical; only the source of the numbers changes.

This is stand-in data, and students should know it. Say so plainly: "We didn't get our matches in, so we're practicing the equation on a pretend season. Next week we do it again with your real bots." The skill transfers; the honesty matters.

How to run it

  1. Open the activity and pick a statUse the same Build Your Bot's Equation → activity. Start with Speed — it's the clearest trend in this dataset (that's by design; the curriculum's win equation is Speed-based).
  2. Give each group two bots to build their lineRead that bot's Speed and Win % off the table and enter them as the two points. Assign different pairs to different groups — that's the whole point: everyone gets a slightly different equation.
  3. Give each group a third bot to testIn Step 4 they plug a third bot's Speed into their equation, compare the predicted win % to the actual, and read the residual. A small non-zero residual is the expected, healthy result.
  4. Compare equations as a classEvery group reads their line aloud. They differ — because two points never capture the whole class. That disagreement is the bridge to the real scatter plot next week.

Answer key

Group A worked example (Tank & Comet, test Rover)

Rate of change (slope): win % change ÷ Speed change = (70 − 30) ÷ (12 − 4) = 40 ÷ 8 = m = 5.

Y-intercept: b = y − m×x, using Tank (4, 30): b = 30 − (5 × 4) = 30 − 20 = b = 10.

Equation: y = 5x + 10  (predicted win % = 5 × Speed + 10).

Test on Rover (Speed 7, actual win % 50): predicted = 5×7 + 10 = 45. Residual = predicted − actual = 45 − 50 = −5. The equation under-predicted by 5 points — Rover did a little better than its Speed alone suggested. A small residual like this is the normal, healthy result.

Group B check: Pip (5, 40) & Bolt (11, 70) → m = 30÷6 = 5, b = 40 − 25 = 15, so y = 5x + 15. Notice A and B found the same slope but a different intercept — good fuel for the class discussion.

What the dataset is built to show

  • Speed is the strong predictor. Win % climbs about 5 points per Speed point, with real scatter around the line — so slopes land near 5–6 and residuals are small but not zero.
  • Endurance runs the other way. In this class the fast bots are the fragile ones, so building a line on Endurance gives a negative slope (e.g. Tank & Comet on Endurance: m = −10). A group that tries a second stat sees that a stat can be negatively linked to winning — a great compare-and-contrast if you have time.
  • Turning and Power barely move. Both cluster around 3–4 for every bot, so they make weak, noisy lines. If a group picks one and gets a wild slope, that's the lesson: a stat with little spread can't predict much.
  • Two points never tell the whole story. Different pairs give different equations from the same eight bots. That's the honest limitation the real scatter plot fixes next week.
