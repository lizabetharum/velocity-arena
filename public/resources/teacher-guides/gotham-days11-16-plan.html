Gotham Tech · Revised plan · Days 11–16

Season 1 in Season 2's place

Because matches haven't run yet, we're dropping Season 2 and giving those days to Season 1. The good news: almost none of the math is lost — Days 13 and 14 carry the win-equation and percent-change lessons either way. What changes is the competition wrapper, not the learning.

Two anchors that cannot move. Day 15 (Wed Jul 29) — Post-Task Diagnostic + Post-Reflection, the program's research data. Day 16 (Thu Jul 30) — Community Exhibition, external guests. Everything else flexes around these two.
The one substitution that saves percent change. Percent change was built on Season 1 → Season 2 allocations. Instead, teams get a single reallocation window on Day 13 — one stat change after seeing Match Day 1 results. That one move buys a real before/after for percent change, keeps the revise-and-retest loop, and gives residuals something to explain.

All times assume the standard 10:00 AM start with a 60-minute lunch.

Plan B If matches still can't run Thursday Day 12 · Jul 23

Decide by 11:00 AM. If bots aren't reliably driving a straight square by the end of the morning block, switch — don't spend the afternoon troubleshooting and end the day with no data.

Run a Skills Ladder instead of head-to-head matches

Teams take turns running a scored course, one bot on the mat at a time. This is dramatically more robust than matches: no opponent to coordinate, no ball chaos, and teams can share working bots if only some are flashed.

  • The course: Skills Arena Challenge 1 (Dead Straight), 3 (Straight Shot), 5 (Corner Kick Right). Three attempts per team.
  • Scoring: 1 point per challenge completed cleanly, +1 bonus if the ball ends in the goal zone. Max 6 per attempt. Call these "goals" — the vocabulary should match the real season.
  • Keep every ritual intact: predict your score before each attempt → run → record actual → gap sentence → loss recovery for any team that scored below its prediction.
  • Post a leaderboard exactly as you would for matches, with Best Prediction named alongside top score.
Why this protects the rest of the week: the Skills Ladder produces the same shape of data as a match log — predicted, actual, error, per team. So Day 13's win equation just becomes Speed vs. Skills Score instead of Speed vs. Goals, and Day 14's residuals and percent change work untouched. Nothing downstream has to change.

If you get bots working later in the week

Switch to real matches on Day 13 or 14 — just label each row in the dashboard as skills or match so the two data sources stay distinguishable. Mixing is fine; unlabeled mixing is not.

If almost nothing is driving

Run the ladder with two shared bots on a rotation and have waiting teams act as scorers and timekeepers — being the official is real work, and it keeps the prediction ritual alive. A short day of real data beats a full day of troubleshooting.
