Gotham Tech · Mon Jul 27 – Thu Jul 30

What To Do, Monday to Thursday

Print this and keep it on the table. Do the steps in order. If you only do the steps on this page, the week works.

Set up once before Monday

There are no matches this week — and that's on purpose
The bots drive fine, but scoring a goal needs stats tuned for it, which is not where this group is — so matches keep coming out 0–0, and a 0–0 tells you nothing. The whole week runs on the Distance Lab instead: drive the bot, measure how far it went, predict the next one. Distance can never come out 0–0 — it needs no opponent and no goal, just a tape measure, so every bot produces a real number every time.
What you need on the tables Monday
A tape measure or metre stick for every team — the most important object in the room — a strip of tape on the floor as a start line, and the Distance Lab worksheet printed (one per team). Load the Distance Lab MakeCode onto every bot: button A drives, button B adds half a second.
Say this before every single run "Write down one number, before the bot moves. How many centimetres will it travel? No ranges. Sign it."
Say this after every single run "We predicted ____. We got ____. The gap was ____. Because ____."

Monday — the Distance Lab Jul 27

  1. 10:00Start straight in. Hand out the worksheet. Say what today is: "We're going to predict how far the bot travels, before it moves, then measure how close we got." Teach the two rituals above.No warm-up video. The bot driving is the hook.
  2. 10:10Each team picks 4 stats that add up to 20. Write every team's stats on the board.These do not change all week. Say that out loud.
  3. 10:20Trial 1 — drive for exactly 1 second, measure how far in cm.That number is the bot's speed in cm per second. It goes at the top of their sheet.
  4. —Trial 2 — predict first. "If we drive 3 seconds we go ___ cm" (their cm-per-second × 3). Then run it and measure.Then the gap sentence. Most bots go further than predicted, and the miss grows the longer they drive — let them find that out, do not tell them.
  5. —Keep going to five trials, and finish the worksheet. They subtract two runs to fix their speed, find the start-up cost, and write their equation distance = speed × time + start-up.Put these three on the board so nobody invents what went wrong: too far / too short (the model is off) · not straight (motors not matched) · different every run (batteries or start position — fix before tuning anything).
  6. 12:00Lunch.
  7. 1:00Make your equation poster in Canva. Your equation and your graph of the five dots. Nothing else yet. Poster worksheet →This is the thing you will teach a visitor on Thursday. Tell them so now.Also write the equation big on a sheet of paper and tape it to the wall — that's what the class compares in tomorrow's Model Swap.
  8. 1:45Introduce the Target Challenge. Put a tape line on the floor at a distance you choose (say 100 cm). "Use your equation to pick the drive time that stops your bot on the line." They compute it, drive, measure the miss. A few tries each.This is the game for the rest of the week — and it is what visitors will try on Thursday.
  9. 2:20Each team says their smallest Target miss out loud. Name tomorrow: "You'll compare your equation with another team's, then get better at the target."
How you know Monday worked
Every team leaves with five measured trials, a written equation, and a poster started. That is the whole bar. Three trials is the honest minimum; five is the target. Do not rush the measuring to reach five.

Bots driving crooked? A team can spend part of the afternoon on Skills Arena Challenge 1 (Dead Straight) instead — a bot that drives straight measures cleaner. Only if they need it; it is not the plan.

Tuesday — compare models, then beat the target Jul 28

  1. 10:00Read the equations on the wall. Ask: "Whose bot is fastest? How can you tell from the equation?"The speed is the number in front of the time. Every team's is different — that is correct, do not fix it.
  2. 10:15MODEL SWAP. Every team's equation is already different — different bots, different speeds. Each team picks one other team, types that team's speed and start-up into the equation tool to see both bots on one graph, then writes: what we keep, what we change, and why.The "why" must use a number — "their speed is 6 cm/s bigger than ours" — not "theirs looks better."
  3. 10:50TARGET CHALLENGE — the real test. New target line at a distance they have never driven to. Predict the drive time from the equation, write it down, then drive.This is the block that proves the equation is worth anything: getting a distance right that you never measured. Run several rounds at new distances.
  4. 12:00Lunch.
  5. 1:00Is your model getting better? Each team fills one card: "Our Target miss started at ____ cm and is now ____ cm. The change that helped most was ____."If a team got worse, that is fine. They write why. Do not fake a happy ending.
  6. 1:45Finish your Canva poster. Add the Target Challenge and the before/after miss. This is now the whole story you'll teach Thursday.

Wednesday — tests first, then get ready to teach Jul 29

  1. 10:00Post-Task Diagnostic. Do this FIRST. 12 minutes, on their own, no talking.Say: "This is not a test of you. We are comparing you to yourself four weeks ago."
  2. 10:15Post-Reflection Survey. Same sitting. Check every name is spelled the same as Day 1.
  3. 10:30Bot lore — add one sentence. Each team adds one line to their bot's story that only makes sense because of what they measured this week. Post it.
  4. 10:45Teach the Math. Each team explains their own equation using their real numbers, as if to someone who has never seen it. "The 38 means our bot goes 38 cm every second. The minus 7 means it loses 7 cm getting started."
  5. 12:00Lunch.
  6. 1:00Finish the station. Poster on the wall, bot ready, tape line down, the Target Challenge set up exactly as a visitor will meet it.
  7. 1:45TEACH-BACK. Team A pretends to be a confused visitor. Team B explains the Target Challenge once. Team A then tries it with no more help.Write down exactly where Team A got stuck. Team B rewrites that one sentence. Then swap.
Do not skip steps 1 and 2 for any reason
The diagnostic and the reflection are the only things this week that cannot be done later. That is why they are at 10:00. If the day runs late, cut poster polish — never these, and never the teach-back.

Thursday — teach it, then celebrate Jul 30

  1. 10:00Set up stations + last rehearsal. Poster, bot, tape line, the sentence they rewrote yesterday.
  2. 10:40Exhibition. Visitors try each team's Target Challenge: the team teaches them to use the equation to hit the line. Fill the tracker.The question is: did the visitor hit the target after one explanation? Stay out of it — the students teach.
  3. 11:50Snack.
  4. 12:10Walk the Wall. Everyone walks past all four days of work in silence. Do not talk over it.
  5. 12:30Closing circle + final journal. Each student names one moment this week they solved something they thought they couldn't. No awards — just the room hearing each other.
  6. 1:30Done.

If this happens, do this

If…Then…
You're tempted to run a real match "just to try." Don't. The bots drive fine, but scoring needs stats tuned for it, so a match will almost certainly come back 0–0 and cost you an hour for no data. The Target Challenge is the scoring skill — precision to a spot — without needing an opponent or a goal, so it cannot come out 0–0. Put the energy there.
A bot won’t move at all. Check in this order: LEDs showing a number? (if not, re-flash) → batteries + the Cutebot’s own power switch → Cutebot Pro extension in the project. If only one or two bots work, share them — each team still writes its own prediction and measures its own trial.
A team's numbers jump around ("different every run"). Do not build an equation on them yet. Fresh batteries, same start line every time, re-measure. A consistent change per second (like 38, 38, 38) means the data is good.
A team finishes the worksheet early Monday. Send them to the Target Challenge early, at a harder distance they have never driven to. Or start their poster. Do not hand them busywork — make the target harder.
You get fewer than 3 trials Monday. Teach the equation using the practice dataset so the method lands, then have teams rebuild it on whatever real numbers they got.
Wednesday is running late. Cut poster polish. Keep the diagnostic, the reflection, and the teach-back. A plain poster with a rehearsed explanation beats a beautiful one nobody practised.
A team says "our equation is wrong." Good. Ask: "What number tells you that?" Then have them write the change on their card. A wrong equation they can explain is worth more than a right one they copied.
