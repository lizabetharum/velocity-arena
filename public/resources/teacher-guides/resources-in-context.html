The program ships with a lot of resources. The question isn't "what's available?" — it's "which one do I reach for, when, and what decision does it help me make?" This page walks the four-week arc from before Day 1 through the closing exhibition and names the resource that belongs at each decision moment.

PHASE 01 Before Day 1 — Get ready. Pre-season prep

Everything in this phase is about closing gaps in your own readiness before you stand in front of students. Three of the four resources here are not things students ever see — they're scaffolding for you.

Am I prepared to run Day 1 without faking it?
Teacher support
Use
Facilitator Prep Checklist ↗

Ten specific items: every formula calculated by memory in under 10 seconds, the four-step loss-recovery script practiced aloud, the room laid out with the wall reserved for Driving Question + Bot Names + Leaderboard + Rivalry Cards.

So you can

Not open Day 1 with anything on this list unfinished. The list exists because the ten most common new-facilitator failures correspond exactly to skipping one of these ten items.

What is this program actually asking students to do, and how will I know it's working?
Teacher support
Use
Toolkit Facilitator Guide ↗ Facilitator Schedule ↗

The four program goals with named evidence statements, the six-block daily structure, room setup, and a day-by-day arc with risk moments flagged.

So you can

Recognize success in real time. The evidence statements ("a residual: predicted vs. actual, named", "an explanation revised after a visitor got stuck") are the look-fors you'll use every block.

Where are my students mathematically before we begin?
Assessment Dashboard tool
Use
Pre-Task Diagnostic ↗ Diagnostic Growth Dashboard ↗

An 8-question, 12-minute baseline covering proportional reasoning, slope, distance, and quadratic thinking — no bot framing. Results land per-concept on the dashboard.

So you can

Decide which Day 1–5 mini-blocks need extra scaffolding for which students — and have a baseline you can compare against on Day 15 to show actual growth, not vibes.

Is the hardware ready to put in students' hands?
Teacher support
Use
MakeCode setup ↗ Launch Sequences ↗

Offline MakeCode install, WebUSB pairing flow, and a reference launch-sequence MakeCode block teams will adapt for Day 6.

So you can

Catch hardware problems before students do. A bot that won't pair on Day 1 burns the trust the Failure Bow is supposed to build.

PHASE 02 Each day — Plan and run the blocks. D 01 → D 16, daily loop

Every day has the same six-block shape (Hook → Challenge → Snack → Match-Build-Make → Open Lab → ENDS). These are the resources you reach for inside the loop, not the ones you read once before the program starts.

What am I running today, in what order, with what timings?
Teacher support
Use
Today ↗ Teacher Schedule ↗

The day's six blocks with durations and the per-activity facilitator description — the same description that's now mirrored across days-default.js, days-tn.js, and facilitator-moves.js.

So you can

Know where to stand at each transition and what the next 30 seconds of the activity must accomplish — instead of reading the script mid-block.

How do I run this specific activity? What are the facilitator moves?
Teacher support
Use
Quick Cards + Scripts (Teacher Schedule) ↗ Cheat Sheet ↗

Each activity links a teacher-only quick card (pink pill) and a full facilitator script (Google Doc). Quick cards are scripted lines, common-error watchlist, and exit-ticket expectations. Cheat sheet collapses all four formulas onto one page.

So you can

Run the activity from the front of the room without paper — and intervene with the right talk-move when a team is stuck, not whatever comes to mind.

What does the student-facing version look like? Am I asking them to do what the lesson asks?
Student support
Use
Worksheet + Lesson links (Teacher Schedule) ↗ Student day view ↗

Every activity in the Teacher Schedule lists the worksheet, the interactive lesson page, and (where it exists) the MakeCode link students will use. The Student day view is the same page students see — no teacher-only fields rendered.

So you can

Pace-check against the lesson, not against your gut. If you can't predict where a team will be at minute 20, re-read the lesson — students may be on a section you haven't seen.

How will I know if a team's work is at the level we expect?
Exemplar
Use
Student Work Exemplars ↗

For most activities, three real student-work samples at Approaching / Meets / Exceeds, with a one-line explanation of what distinguishes each level. Also surfaced inline in the Teacher Schedule as purple Exemplar pills next to each activity.

So you can

Push a team using a specific language move: "Your work looks like the Meets sample — what's missing to make it Exceeds?" Better than a generic "add more detail."

How do students first enter a loadout and gap sentence into the dashboard? (Days 1–5)
Dashboard tool Teacher support
Use
Pre-Season Dashboard Guide ↗ Dashboard Intro Script ↗

Pre-Season guide walks through the Days 1–5 workflow: stat cards, the first official allocation, dashboard loadout entry, and the three-part gap sentence (formula predicted / bot did / gap). Intro Script is the 12–15 min Day 5 walkthrough that lands all teams in the dashboard the same way.

So you can

Lock in the data-entry standard before Season 1 begins. Every team enters loadouts the same way and writes gap sentences in the same format — so when Season 1 matches start, residuals are comparable instead of each team using its own shorthand.

A team is stuck on a specific math concept — where do I send them (or take the whole room)?
Student support Teacher support
Use
Interactive Math Concepts ↗ Math Playground ↗

Thirteen browser-based concept pages — Unit Rate, Ratio, Proportional Scaling, Linear Decay, Coordinate Geometry, Arc Approximation, Slope, Budget, Percent Change, Graphs, Linear Equations, Data & Residuals — plus a Playground that runs all eight core visualizations on one canvas for board demos.

So you can

Hand a stuck team a self-contained interactive instead of a re-explanation — they manipulate it, you keep moving. Use Playground when you need a quick whole-room visualization (e.g. "watch what happens to Charge% when I drag Power from 4 to 8").

PHASE 03 During a match — Predict, run, recover. Match days · D 06 → D 15

The match itself isn't the assessment — the loop around it is. Prediction before the bots move, residual calculation after, and a structured recovery for the team that lost.

How do I run the pre-match prediction ritual and the post-match residual entry?
Dashboard tool Teacher support
Use
In-Season Dashboard Guide ↗

Walks through the match-day workflow across Seasons 1 and 2: equation-based prediction before the match, real-time match logging, residual calculation, and the gap-sentence entry that ties each match back to the team's model.

So you can

Require a written prediction before any bot moves — the rule that converts a match from "drama" into "data."

A team wants to try a risky loadout — can I let them preview it without losing a match?
Dashboard tool
Use
Match Simulator ↗

Cutebot Pro Soccer simulation: enter two stat loadouts, run a match, see goal/time/residual outcomes. Useful in Open Lab and Commissioner's Week reallocation.

So you can

Direct teams toward bench-testing instead of live testing when stakes are high (Season 1 final, Championship), and surface the loadouts most worth running on the field.

A team just lost badly and is shutting down — what now?
Teacher support Student support
Use
Loss Recovery Protocol ↗

A four-step script. Step 1 is 30 seconds of silence after naming the feeling. Step 2 is the dashboard. Step 3 is the next match. The script is the resource — say it the way it's written.

So you can

Hand a frustrated team a specific math move instead of generic reassurance. Run it for every losing team, every time — not just the ones who visibly need it.

What does a "good" residual analysis look like before I judge a team's?
Exemplar
Use
Day 10 / 13 / 14 residual exemplars ↗

Three sample residual analyses per activity. The Exceeds samples typically name which equation parameter (slope vs intercept) the residual implicates; the Approaching samples stop at "the bot was off."

So you can

Pick the team to call on for share-out — and know in advance whether their finding will move the room forward or stall it.

PHASE 04 End of week — Are teams tracking? Friday + season transitions

Every Friday you make the same call: who's behind, on what concept, and what fits inside next week to address it. The dashboard is the system that tells you, and Cold Recall (Day 10) is the formal check.

Who's behind, on what, by how much?
Dashboard tool
Use
Live Dashboard ↗ Leaderboard ↗

Per-team match history, prediction accuracy, residual size and direction. The Best Prediction column tracks model accuracy independent of win/loss, so a team that loses with tight residuals is visible.

So you can

Pick the one or two teams to target in next week's Open Labs — and decide whether the gap is a model problem, a code problem, or a hardware problem.

Did the class actually retain the math, or did they fake it during instruction?
Assessment Student support
Use
Day 10 Cold Recall Check ↗ Math Concepts (re-teach surface) ↗

Five questions, individual, no notes, no devices, no grade. The point isn't to score it — it's to tally who can still write the Endurance decay or Power² formula cold. When the tally shows a class-wide gap, the matching interactive concept page (Linear Decay, Proportional Scaling, etc.) is the re-teach.

So you can

Fold the re-teach into the next Challenge Block with specific names attached — and send students to the concept page in Open Lab instead of re-lecturing.

What do I actually do at the Commissioner's Meeting and Vote?
Teacher support
Use
Day 12 facilitator script + reference doc ↗

3-minute presentation format, math-verification protocol, the rule that an objection only counts if it names a number and a calculation showing differential impact. The reference doc (teacher-only) holds the worked examples.

So you can

Dismiss "I don't like it" arguments without a number and hold the room to the standard the rest of the program has built.

PHASE 05 End of program — Did it work? Day 15 + Day 16

Two summative measures: the post-task diagnostic (math you can measure) and the visitor exhibition (math students can teach). Both produce reportable evidence.

Did students learn what we said they would?
Assessment Dashboard tool
Use
Post-Task Diagnostic ↗ Growth Dashboard ↗

Parallel-form post-diagnostic on Day 15. The Growth Dashboard shows per-concept percent change vs. Day 1 baseline, anonymized.

So you can

Report concrete growth to stakeholders — and identify which concept(s) underperformed for the next cohort's redesign.

Can students explain the math to someone who's never seen the program?
Assessment
Use
Day 16 Community Exhibition ↗

Each visitor attempts three mini-challenges (unit rate, coordinate distance, proportional scaling) after the student's explanation. A Visitor Completion Tracker logs first-try completions per team.

So you can

Score Goal 4 ("the ability to teach") as a binary observable — did a stranger complete all three challenges on the first try? — rather than a judgment call.

What story do I tell at the closing ceremony — what did the program actually produce?
Teacher support
Use
Walk the Wall + Awards Ceremony ↗

Every artifact from four weeks on the wall — bot names, leaderboard, Commissioner rules, scouting reports. The Awards Ceremony script names each award with its data criterion (e.g. "Most Improved — the highest Season 1 → Season 2 win-rate percent change") so the math stays visible in the celebration.

So you can

Close on what the program produced — not a single winner, but a room of students who can explain algebra using their own data.

I want to get better at this. Where do I review my own moves before the next cohort?
Teacher support
Use
Failure Modes ↗ Experience Map ↗ Battlefield Cards ↗

Three reflective toolkit pages aimed at the facilitator, not the student. Failure Modes catalogs the moves that quietly sink a session (rescuing too early, dropping the exit ticket, narrating the gallery walk). Experience Map traces the emotional arc across the 16 days so you can see which moment a struggle actually belongs to. Battlefield Cards are the talk-moves and recovery scripts in print-and-keep-in-pocket form.

So you can

Name what didn't work and pick one change for next time — instead of relying on vague "I should run that differently." Between-cohort reflection is the only place these resources land; during a session you reach for the scripts instead.
