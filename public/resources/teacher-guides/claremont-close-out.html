4-Day Close-Out

The last four days of the program — one per day, 10:00–3:00 (one hour for lunch). This class has driving robots and finished the stat labs, so these days run the Distance Lab: students drive, measure, and build a real equation from their own numbers — no matches needed. Do the steps in order; each has a clock time. Have ready daily: robots, student devices, and paper/pencils.

Two things that must happen — do not skip

1. Friday: every student takes the Post-Task and the Reflection online. The pilot's whole measurement against Day 1. Open the Post-Task (~12 min, silent), then the Reflection. Each student types name, last initial, and team exactly as on Day 1, or their results can't be matched. Do it even if you run out of time for everything else.

2. Students teach their own math to a visitor. Do not answer for them.

Distance Lab + Target Challenge Day 1 · 10–3

Also have ready: a tape measure per team, painter's tape for start/target lines. Guides: Distance Lab · Answer Key.

  1. 10:00Set up the Distance Lab. Tape a start line per team. Load the Distance Lab code (A drives, B adds 0.5 s). Teach: predict BEFORE the bot moves; after each run say "we predicted ___, we got ___, the gap was ___."
  2. 10:20Distance Lab worksheet. Drive 1 s → speed. Predict a longer drive, measure, fix ONE number. Five trials, then write distance = speed × time + start-up.Student sheet: EN · ES.
  3. 12:00Lunch (1 hour).
  4. 1:00Target Challenge — Precision Final. Tape a target line. Solve the drive time from your equation, ×1000 to ms, program, drive. Practice, then a surprise target — closest wins.Sheet: EN · ES · MakeCode · Answer Key.
  5. 2:15Start the equation poster in Canva: your equation and a graph of your trial dots. Finish it tomorrow.How-to: EN · ES.

Percent change + finish the poster Day 2 · 10–3

  1. 10:00Percent Change. How much did your prediction improve? "First guess off by ___, best off by ___ — a ___% improvement."Help: Percent Change.
  2. 10:45Finish the poster + QR code. Add the percent-change result. Make a QR to the shared Canva design. Print posters in black & white on 8.5 × 11 — the QR opens the full-color design in Canva.
  3. 12:00Lunch (1 hour).
  4. 1:00Compare equations. Read another team's equation; write one thing to keep and one to re-check in yours — citing a number.
  5. 1:45Skills Arena + catch-up. Finish any lab work, then the Skills Arena driving challenges (drive straight, exact angles, park on a spot).

Practice teaching Day 3 · 10–3

  1. 10:00Teach the Math. Each team explains its equation in plain language with its real numbers — what the speed number means, what the start-up means. Write your one-sentence opener.Tool: Teach the Math.
  2. 11:00Teach-back rehearsal. Pair up. One is a confused visitor; the other explains ONCE and lets them try — no rescuing. Fix the sentence that didn't land, then switch.
  3. 12:00Lunch (1 hour).
  4. 1:00Set up your exhibition station. Poster up, bot ready, target line down — staged the way a visitor will meet it. Do one full dry run.
  5. 2:00Skills Arena / polish. Ready teams practice Skills Arena or polish their station.

Teach visitors, then measure the growth Day 4 · Friday · 10–3

  1. 10:00Expert setup + last rehearsal. Stations ready; run the sentence you rewrote yesterday once more.
  2. 10:30Community Exhibition. Visitors try your challenge and you teach them; fill the Visitor Handout — did they get it after ONE explanation? Stay out of it; the students teach.Handout: EN · ES.
  3. 12:00Lunch (1 hour).
  4. 1:00Post-Task Diagnostic + Reflection. 12 min, individual, silent — then the Reflection. Names must match Day 1. Protect this; never cut it.Post-Task · Reflection.
  5. 1:50Closing circle + celebrate. Each student names one thing they can do now that they couldn't on Day 1. Sign posters, group photo, send-off.
What you are NOT doing — and that's fine

No matches, no championship, no scouting, no awards. This class never ran matches, and that's okay — the Distance Lab gives the same real math (build an equation, test it, improve it, teach it). If a link won't open or something is missing, contact the site coordinator.
