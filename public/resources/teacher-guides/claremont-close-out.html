4-Day Close-Out

These are the last four days of the program — one session per day, ending Friday. Do the steps in order. Each step tells you what to do, about how long it takes, and what students should have when they finish. If you only do what is on this page, the program finishes properly. Have ready each day: the student devices/tablets, the robots, and paper/pencils.

Two things that must happen — do not skip

1. Friday: every student takes the Post-Task and the Reflection online. This is the most important part of the whole week. Open the Post-Task on each device (about 12 minutes, quiet, on their own), then the Reflection. Each student must type their name, last initial, and team exactly the way they did on Day 1 — otherwise their results can't be matched. Do this even if you run out of time for everything else.

2. Students teach their own math to a visitor. Do not answer for them. Your job is to keep it moving, not to explain the math.

Finish the matches, write the equation Session 1 · Today

  1. ~45 min. Run the last robot matches the same way the class has done them all week.
  2. ~30 min. Each team writes their equation on paper and fills in this one sentence: "Our first guess was off by ____. Our best guess was off by ____. That is a ____% improvement."If they need help with the percent, open the Percent Change page on the screen.
  3. ~15 min. Each team reads one other team's equation and writes: one thing we would keep, one thing we would change, and the number that made us decide.Done when: every team has a written equation and the percent sentence filled in.

Change one rule Session 2

  1. ~40 min. Each team suggests ONE change to the game rules. They must say two numbers from their own data and who the change would help. As a class, vote ONE change in.Use the Rule Proposal page for the format.
  2. ~10 min. Each student picks the ONE thing they will teach a visitor on Friday and writes it down.Done when: one rule is voted in, and every student has written what they will teach.

Practice teaching Session 3

  1. ~20 min. Each team sets up their station: their equation/poster, their robot, and the game or target set up the way a visitor will see it.
  2. ~30 min. Students pair up and practice. One student is the "confused visitor." The other explains their math once and lets the visitor try — no helping. Then they fix the part that was confusing and switch roles.Done when: every student has practiced their explanation at least once and fixed the confusing part.

Teach for real, then the quiz Session 4 · Friday

  1. ~45 min. Visitors come in (parents, staff, or another class). Students teach them the math and let them try the challenges. Mark the Visitor Handout: did the visitor get it after one explanation? Do not answer for the students.
  2. ~20 min. POST-TASK + REFLECTION (do not skip this). Open the Post-Task on every device — 12 minutes, quiet, on their own — then the Reflection. Names must match Day 1.Say: "This is not a test of you. We are comparing you to yourself four weeks ago."
  3. ~10 min. Closing circle. Each student says one thing they can do now that they could not do on Day 1.
You are NOT doing these — and that's fine

No championship tournament, no extra match days, no scouting reports, no awards ceremony. Those are optional. The four sessions above are the parts that count. If a link won't open or something is missing, contact the site coordinator.
