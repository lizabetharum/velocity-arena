Velocity Arena · Claremont International HS

4-Day Close-Out

The last four days of the program — one session per day, ending Friday. Do the steps in order. This class has driving robots and finished the stat labs, so these days use the Distance Lab: students drive a bot, measure how far it goes, and build a real math equation from their own numbers — no matches needed. Each step says what to do, about how long, and what students should have when they finish. Have ready every day: the robots, student devices/tablets, and paper/pencils.

Two things that must happen — do not skip

1. Friday: every student takes the Post-Task and the Reflection online. This is the most important part of the whole week. Open the Post-Task on each device (about 12 minutes, quiet, on their own), then the Reflection. Each student must type their name, last initial, and team exactly the way they did on Day 1, or their results can't be matched. Do this even if you run out of time for everything else.

2. Students teach their own math to a visitor. Do not answer for them — keep it moving, but let them explain.

Build the distance equation Session 1 · Today

Also have ready: one tape measure (or meter stick) per team, and painter's tape for a start line. Facilitator guides: Distance Lab · Answer Key.

  1. ~15 min. Set up. Put a strip of tape on the floor as a start line for each team. Load the Distance Lab code onto each bot (button A drives; button B adds half a second). The bot's back wheels start behind the tape every time.
  2. ~60 min. Students do the Distance Lab worksheet. Drive 1 second, measure how far in centimeters (that is the bot's speed). Predict a longer drive, run it, measure, fix the number. Five trials. They end with an equation: distance = speed × time + start-up.Say before every run: "Write your prediction down BEFORE the bot moves. How many centimeters?"
  3. ~15 min. Each team writes their final equation big on paper.Done when: every team has five measured trials and a written equation.

Hit the target, measure the improvement Session 2

  1. ~50 min. Target Challenge. Tape a target line on the floor. Each team uses their equation backward to find the drive time that stops the bot on the line, turns it into milliseconds (× 1000), programs it, and drives. A few practice targets, then a surprise target — closest to the line wins.Student sheet: Target Challenge. Code: Target Challenge code · MakeCode.
  2. ~20 min. Percent Change. Each team figures out how much their prediction improved: "Our first guess was off by ____. Our best was off by ____. That is a ____% improvement."Help page: Percent Change. Done when: every team hit at least one target and wrote its percent.

Make the poster, practice teaching Session 3

  1. ~40 min. Each team makes a poster (paper or Canva): their equation and a graph of their trial dots.How-to: Make Your Equation Poster.
  2. ~30 min. Practice teaching. Students pair up. One is the "confused visitor." The other explains their math once and lets the visitor try — no helping. Then they fix the confusing part and switch.Done when: every student has a poster and has practiced explaining once.

Teach for real, then the quiz Session 4 · Friday

  1. ~45 min. Visitors come in (parents, staff, or another class). Students teach them to hit the target line using their equation, and let them try. Mark the Visitor Handout (ES): did the visitor get it after one explanation? Do not answer for the students.
  2. ~20 min. POST-TASK + REFLECTION (do not skip). Open the Post-Task on every device — 12 minutes, quiet, on their own — then the Reflection. Names must match Day 1.Say: "This is not a test of you. We are comparing you to yourself four weeks ago."
  3. ~10 min. Closing circle. Each student says one thing they can do now that they could not do on Day 1.
If students finish early

Send them to Skills Arena — the robot driving challenges (drive straight, turn exact angles, park on a spot). Great practice that also sharpens their Distance Lab driving. It has an EN | ES toggle in the top corner.

What you are NOT doing — and that's fine

No matches, no championship, no scouting, no awards. This class never ran matches, and that's okay — the Distance Lab gives the same real math (build an equation, test it, improve it, teach it) without needing them. If a link won't open or something is missing, contact the site coordinator.
