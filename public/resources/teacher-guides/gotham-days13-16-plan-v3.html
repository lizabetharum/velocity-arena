Gotham Tech · Plan v3 · Mon Jul 27 – Thu Jul 30

The whole arc in four days

Four weeks of Velocity Arena is really one loop run many times: predict → run → measure the gap → change one thing → predict again. The calendar was never the point; the number of times through the loop is. This plan runs that loop about ten times in four days and attaches one piece of evidence to each project goal. It is built on Shavonne's team's outline, with three additions that the goals require.

The single principle behind this plan. Do not try to re-teach four weeks of math. Pick one model (a linear equation from their own match data), and put it through the loop enough times that students can point to a change they made and the improvement it caused. Depth of the loop beats coverage of topics — and it is what all four goals actually ask for.

Three additions to the Monday–Thursday outline, and why

The outline (Mon collect · Tue analyze · Wed assess + prep · Thu present) is the right shape and this plan keeps it. But two of the four goals cannot be evidenced by that outline as written, so three blocks are added:

1 · Tuesday afternoon: Proving Rounds (the important one). In the outline, Tuesday ends with teams changing their inputs and predicting what will happen next — and then the week moves on to assessments. But Goal 1 asks students to point to a change that reduced the gap in later matches. If the revised prediction is never tested on the field, there are no later matches and the goal cannot be met. So Tuesday afternoon runs three more live rounds using equation-based predictions. This is what turns a guess into a model.
2 · Tuesday 11:15: Model Swap. Goal 2 requires a documented comparison to another team's model and a specific keep/change decision. Students will not do this spontaneously — they will assume the other team is either right or irrelevant. It needs a scheduled 30 minutes and a slip of paper.
3 · Wednesday afternoon: teach-back rehearsal. Goal 4's bar is high — a visitor completes all three mini-challenges after one explanation. No team clears that bar on the first attempt. They need one rehearsal against a genuinely confused human, and a chance to rewrite the sentence that failed.
Also moved: the Post-Task Diagnostic and Post-Reflection now run first thing Wednesday rather than late in the day. They are the pilot's before/after research measurement and the one thing that cannot be recovered later. Doing them at 10:00 on fresh minds guarantees they happen; anything scheduled after a championship is at the mercy of the clock.

Tuesday Turn the data into a model — then prove it Tue · Jul 28

The heart of the week. Morning: build and compare models. Afternoon: test them on the field. Goals 1 and 2 both land today.

TimeBlockWhat happens
10:00 – 10:15Hook — the Residual BoardRead yesterday's board aloud. Ask the question that frames the day: "Whose predictions got better as the day went on, and why?" Name the Best Prediction team, not just the most wins.
10:15 – 11:15★ Build your equationClass scatter plot from Monday's real results — one stat on x, goals (or win %) on y. Each team picks two of their own points, finds the rate of change, solves for b, and writes Goals = m · Stat + b. Every team's equation will differ. That is the point, not a problem.
11:15 – 11:45★ MODEL SWAP (Goal 2)Equations go up on the wall. Each team studies one other team's equation and fills a Model Swap slip:
① Their equation is ____. ② One thing we are keeping in ours, and why. ③ One thing we are changing, and why — you must cite a number from the data.
Teams may keep everything — but they must defend it with a number, not a shrug.
11:45 – 12:00Revise + predict from the equationApply the keep/change decision. Then use the equation to write predictions for this afternoon's three rounds. Prediction source is no longer "estimate" — it is "our equation."

If a team's gap got worse, that is a legitimate and interesting result — they write why they think so. Do not stage a happy ending.

Plan B If the bots still will not run on Monday decide by 12:00

Do not spend Monday afternoon troubleshooting. If you cannot get three clean rounds by lunch, switch — the week still works.

  • Run a Skills Ladder instead of head-to-head. One bot on the mat at a time, scored course (Skills Arena Challenges 1, 3, 5), three attempts each. Teams can share working bots. Keep every ritual: predict your score → run → record → gap sentence → loss recovery.
  • Why this protects the week: a Skills Ladder produces the same shape of data as a match log — predicted, actual, gap, per team. Tuesday's equation just becomes Stat vs. Skills Score instead of Stat vs. Goals. Nothing downstream changes, including the Proving Rounds.
  • If almost nothing drives: run the ladder on two shared bots and use the practice dataset for Tuesday morning's method. Be honest with students that the numbers are stand-ins, and have them redo it on whatever real data they get.

What we are deliberately not doing

Four days cannot hold four weeks. These are cut on purpose, so nobody spends Tuesday morning trying to squeeze them in:

  • Proportional scaling, decay equations, percent change as standalone lessons. Percent change survives only as the before/after gap calculation on Tuesday afternoon, where it is doing real work.
  • Season 2, the Commissioner's Meeting, Rule Proposals, the Underdog Mechanic. Mark Rule Proposals as N/A for Gotham on the submission checklist so it is not chased at the end.
  • Bot Lore, Rivalry Cards, Four Views. Their function — public commitment and reading your own data — is carried by the Residual Board and the posters.
  • Breadth of topics generally. One model, run through the loop ten times, taught to a stranger. That is the whole plan.
