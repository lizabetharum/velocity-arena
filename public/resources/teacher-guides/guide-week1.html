Week 1 — Build the Machine

Days 1–4 · Speed · Endurance · Turning · Power
Pre-Diagnostic Day 1 Bot Naming Ceremony Day 2 4 Stats Introduced Simulation Lab Daily
Week 1 at a Glance

Week 1 introduces one stat per day. Each day follows the same arc: Hook connects the stat to real-world context, Challenge Block teaches the formula, Match-Build-Make gives students time to code their bot with the new stat, Open Lab runs Simulation Lab challenges. The math is accessible — the goal is fluency with the formula, not mastery yet. Mastery comes from using it repeatedly across all 16 days.

The most important thing you do in Week 1: Hold the standard that every claim requires a number. Students will say "our bot is fast." You say "What does your Speed value give you for Motor%?" Do this from Day 1 and you won't have to fight it in Week 3.
Week 1 Stat Formulas
Speed (S)
Introduced Day 1
Motor% = S ÷ 20 × 100
Range: 1–17 (leaving at least 1 pt for the other 3 stats)
S = 10 → Motor% = 50%. S = 20 → Motor% = 100% (maximum). S = 5 → Motor% = 25%. Higher Speed drains budget because every point costs 1 from the total of 20.
Endurance (E)
Introduced Day 2
P(t) = 100 − (20−E) × 1.5 × t
Range: 1–17  |  t = time in minutes
E = 15 at t=2: P = 100−(5×1.5×2) = 85%. E = 5 at t=2: P = 100−(15×1.5×2) = 55%. Low Endurance bots fade quickly in long matches.
Turning (T)
Introduced Day 3
Ratio = 0.2 + T × 0.02
Range: 1–17
T = 10 → Ratio = 0.40. T = 15 → Ratio = 0.50. The Ratio sets the left motor as a fraction of the right motor speed. Higher T = tighter, faster turns. Low T = wide, slow turns.
Power (P)
Introduced Day 4
Charge% = P²    Follow-Through = P × 200
Range: 1–10 (note: NOT 1–20)
P = 5: Charge = 25%, Follow-Through = 1000ms. P = 10: Charge = 100%, Follow-Through = 2000ms. P is bounded at 10 because P² grows fast — P=10 already maxes charge.

Budget note: Budget = 20 for all teams. Students have NOT been asked to allocate all 4 stats yet — that happens Day 5. In Week 1, they explore each stat independently. The constraint S + P + E + T = 20 is introduced on Day 5.

Day by Day — Week 1
Day 1 · Week 1
Speed — How Fast Can Your Bot Move?
Pre-Diagnostic Today
Session Flow
  • 35 minHook: Circle Pass and Mirror Pairs. trust games.
  • 15 minHook: Pre-diagnostic. Not a student test. No grades. Just a baseline. Keep stakes low.
  • 55 minChallenge Block: Introduce Speed stat. Teach Motor% formula. Students calculate Motor% for 5 sample S values. Class builds a table together.
  • 20-60 minSnack/Lunch Break: Full 20-minute reset.
  • 80 minMatch-Build-Make: Students choose an S value and code Motor% into their bot. Run timed speed trials. Log results on Day 1 worksheet.
  • 20 minOpen Lab: Simulation Lab Challenge — Speed. Students test 3 different S values and record Motor% for each. Discuss trade-offs.
Risk: Diagnostic shame. Students who blank on the pre-diagnostic need identity protection. Redirect any comparison talk. Frame every question as information the program needs, not evidence of what the student doesn't know.
Math Focus
Motor% = S ÷ 20 × 100
Connect to percent: this is just a percent conversion. S out of a maximum of 20, expressed as a percent. Students who struggle with percent can anchor to this formula throughout the program.
Key Questions to Ask
  • "If S = 20, what's Motor%? What does that mean for the bot?"
  • "Which S value gives Motor% = 75%? How do you know?"
  • "If you raise S by 4, what happens to Motor%?"
  • "What's the trade-off of maxing out Speed?"
Simulation Lab: Speed Challenge Test S = 5, 10, 15. Record Motor% for each. Which Speed value let you win the timed trial? Why?
Day 2 · Week 1
Endurance — How Long Can Your Bot Last?
Bot Naming Ceremony
Session Flow
  • 25 minHook: Failure Bow, Slow down, slow down. Trust games.
  • 50 minChallenge Block: Introduce Endurance formula P(t). Students compute power remaining at t=1, t=2, t=3 for E=10 and E=5. Draw the decay curve.
  • 20-60 minSnack/Lunch Break
  • 70 minMatch-Build-Make: Code Endurance into bot. Run endurance trial (match length increases). Log power decay observations.
  • 40 minMatch-Build-Make: Bot Naming.
Milestone: Bot Naming

Bot names are permanent for all 16 days. They go on the wall immediately. The process establishes team identity — treat it with weight. Teams who feel ownership of their bot's name engage more persistently with the math when results are frustrating.

Math Focus
P(t) = 100 − (20−E) × 1.5 × t
This is a linear decay model. (20−E) is the decay rate per minute. The lower the E, the steeper the drop. At E=20, the decay is zero — but that's the entire budget, leaving nothing for other stats.
Key Questions to Ask
  • "What does P(t) equal when E=20? Why is that a problem?"
  • "At E=10 and t=3, what percent power remains?"
  • "Which costs more battery — high Speed or low Endurance?"
  • "If you want P(2) ≥ 70%, what's the minimum E value?"
Simulation Lab: Endurance Challenge Compare E=5 vs E=10 over a 3-minute match. Calculate P(t) at each minute. Which E value keeps the bot above 40% power through the whole match?
Day 3 · Week 1
Turning — Precision Navigation
Ratio Formula
Session Flow
  • 0–25 minHook: Human Robot Programming Challenge. Programming exercise.
  • 75 minChallenge Block: Distance-Time Graphs and Introduction to Turning Ratio formula. Compute Ratio for T=1, 5, 10, 15, 20. Discuss what Ratio values mean physically for motor control.
  • 20-60 minSnack/Lunch Break
  • 90 minMatch-Build-Make: Launch sequence and code Turning into bot. Run navigation course. Students try at least two T values and compare turn radius.
  • 25 minOpen Lab: Simulation Lab — Turning Challenge. Design a path that requires specific turn radius. Teams calculate required T value before testing.
Watch for: Teams who set T very high without understanding the trade-off. "I want it to turn fast" doesn't mean T=17. Press: "What does T=17 cost you in budget? What other stats drop?"
Math Focus
Ratio = 0.2 + T × 0.02
The Ratio is applied to the left motor as a fraction of right motor speed. T=1 gives Ratio=0.22 (very slow left motor = wide turn). T=20 gives Ratio=0.60 (left motor at 60% of right = tight turn).
Key Questions to Ask
  • "T=10 gives what Ratio? What does that mean physically?"
  • "Why does the formula start at 0.2 instead of 0?"
  • "What T value produces a Ratio of exactly 0.50?"
  • "If you need tight turns, should T be high or low?"
Simulation Lab: Turning Challenge Navigate a 3-gate course. Calculate required Ratio for each gate turn. Work backward to find the T value. Test it. Log the difference between predicted and actual turn performance.
Day 4 · Week 1
Power — Strike Force
Power Formulas · End of Week 1
Session Flow
  • 20 minHook:Finger Speed Sums. Timed game.
  • 50 minChallenge Block: Introduce Power formulas. Compute Charge% and Follow-Through for P = 1, 3, 5, 7, 10. Why is P bounded at 10? Discuss the exponential nature of Charge%.
  • 20-60 minSnack/Lunch Break
  • 95–165 minMatch-Build-Make: Scoop Build + Ratio Testing. Build a cardboard scoop. Code Power into bot. Test two P values. Log Charge% and Follow-Through for each trial.
  • 40 minOpen Lab: Free Code Time. Teams refine scoop design and Power speed value in MakeCode. Require one scoop redesign and one speed change per team; teams record which had the bigger effect on kick distance. This is where teams start to internalize the physical-vs-code trade-off.
Preview the budget problem: End Day 4 by asking "Now that you know all 4 stats, what would you choose if your total budget was exactly 20?" This seeds Day 5 thinking without requiring answers yet.
Math Focus
Charge% = P²    Follow-Through = P × 200
Two linked formulas. Charge% is the power drawn from the motor (quadratic). Follow-Through is the duration in milliseconds. P=10 gives 100% charge and 800ms push — but P takes budget from S, E, and T.
Key Questions to Ask
  • "P=7: what's Charge%? What's Follow-Through?"
  • "Why does Charge% grow faster than Follow-Through as P increases?"
  • "P is bounded at 10. Why can't it be 15 or 20 like the other stats?"
  • "If you want Follow-Through ≥ 400ms, what's the minimum P?"
Simulation Lab: Power Challenge Score 3 balls with P=4, then 3 balls with P=8. Record Charge% and Follow-Through for each. Which P value scored more? By how much? Write a complete gap sentence.
Week 1 Simulation Lab Completion Checklist

By end of Day 4, every team should have completed all four challenges.

  • Day 1 — Speed Challenge: tested 3 S values, recorded Motor% for each, wrote gap sentence comparing predicted and actual trial speed
  • Day 2 — Endurance Challenge: compared E=5 vs E=15 over 3 minutes, calculated P(t) at each minute, identified minimum E to stay above 60% power
  • Day 3 — Turning Challenge: navigated 3-gate course, worked backward from required Ratio to T value, logged predicted vs actual turn performance
  • Day 4 — Power Challenge: scored 3 balls at two different P values, recorded Charge% and Follow-Through for each, wrote a complete gap sentence with all 3 parts
Week 1 Critical Risks

Pre-Diagnostic Shame (Day 1)

The pre-diagnostic surfaces academic identity. Students who "don't do math" will disengage at Day 1 if the diagnostic feels like a verdict. Frame it as: "This is just to see where you are now, so we can see where you end up. No grades" Don't discuss results publicly.

Formula-as-Rule Instead of Formula-as-Reasoning

Students who memorize Motor% = S ÷ 20 × 100 without understanding it can't apply it when parameters change. Ask "why" questions, not just "calculate" questions. Mastery = being able to explain the formula's meaning, not just its arithmetic.

Single-Stat Fixation

Teams fall in love with one stat (usually Speed) and max it out without thinking about the other three. Day 4's closing question seeds the trade-off problem. If a team has already decided Speed = 20, they won't think about budget on Day 5 — probe them now.

Bot Naming Underweighted

The naming activity is not filler. Teams who invest in a bot name persist longer through losses in Weeks 2–3. Give it real time, post names visibly on the wall, and reference bot names by name throughout the program.
