Launch-and-Score — Format

Day 14 (Tuesday) · the friendly competition after teams build their launch · most goals of 5

After the Launch Sequence, teams put it to the test. A team's launch is their distance equation running the robot — corner → midline → ball, drive times from ms = (distance − start-up) ÷ speed × 1000. Whoever measured well and computed clean numbers scores more. This is the model paying off, and it cannot come out 0–0: the ball is stationary and the path is programmed.

Set the field — the same for everyone

One field, one setup

Mark one start corner, one ball spot, and set the goal width with tape. Every team launches from the identical setup — that's what makes it fair. Keep the distance modest and the goal generous; you want scoring, not frustration.

A team that starts from the right corner turns LeftInPlace first; from the left, RightInPlace. Pick one corner for the whole event so nobody re-measures.

Same corner, ball spot, and goal for every team.

How a turn works

  1. Warm-up (a few minutes each): every team gets 2–3 practice launches to zero in — adjust the four numbers, re-download.
  2. Scored round: each team takes 5 launches. A launch is a goal when the ball fully crosses the goal line.
  3. Re-tuning is allowed between launches — change your drive times / angles and re-download. The launch is yours to improve; that's the point.
  4. One neutral ref (you, or a rotating student not on the launching team) calls goal / no-goal at the line. Reset the ball on its spot and the bot in the corner between launches.
  5. Winner: most goals out of 5.
  6. Tie: one sudden-death launch each — closest to the centre of the goal wins (ref measures). Repeat if still tied.
Kick not landing? Do a quick kick test first — wide goal, short distance, ball a few cm ahead — before the scored round. If a team's kick won't land at all, they compete on closest approach (ball nearest the goal line) instead of goals, so they still have a fair turn while they keep tuning.
Tie it back to the math

After the round, ask the top team: "Which of your four numbers did you change last, and why did it help?" The answer should name a distance and a drive time — the equation, not luck.
