⚠ Common Error
Absolute change (9 − 6 = 3) and percent change ((6 − 9) ÷ 9 × 100 = −33.3%) are not the same. The objection window always uses percent change. A drop of 3 points means nothing until you know what percent of the original value that is.

✓  Worked Example — Read This First. Do Not Skip.
Team Thunder proposes: "Speed may not exceed 6 points in Season 2." They cite two data points: (1) their current Speed = 9, (2) the last-place team's Speed = 3. Use their numbers to check the math before looking at the answer.
▲ Wrong — Absolute Change
Old Speed: 9   New cap: 6
Change = 9 − 6 = 3 points
"Speed drops by 3" — this is absolute change. It tells you nothing about proportion. A 3-point drop from 9 is very different from a 3-point drop from 5.
✓ Correct — Percent Change
Old Speed: 9   New cap: 6
(6 − 9) ÷ 9 × 100 = −33.3%
"Speed drops by 33.3%" — now you can compare across teams. If the median drop is ~10%, a 33% drop is a valid objection.

Teacher Look-For — Approaching (Marty T.)
Marty correctly identifies the two data points in Steps 1 and 2 for every presentation — the numerator (new – old) is always right. His single consistent error is in Step 3: he divides by the new value (the cap) instead of the old value (the current stat). This makes all his percent changes larger in magnitude than the correct answer. The most dangerous consequence: Presentation 3 had the same error Marty does, so his wrong answer matched the team's wrong answer — he validated incorrect math as correct. In the tracker, the same inversion flips the shortcut, making him believe Voltage is hurt by a Speed cap when Voltage's Speed is already below that cap. Ask him: "In the formula (new – old) ÷ old, what number are you dividing by — and in Presentation 1, is that 8 or 7?" If he says 7, point to the 'Old value (current stat)' box in Section F and ask him to trace which team stat that refers to.
