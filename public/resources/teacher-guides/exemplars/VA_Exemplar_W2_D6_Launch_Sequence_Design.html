Launch Sequence Design — precision vs reliability

Students design two launch strategies, test each three times, and choose the one that works every time, not the one that works best once. The low sample picks the single best hit. The high sample reads the spread and ties the landing spot to the autonomous phase. Illustrative samples, built before the pilot.

Teacher look-for
He runs both strategies three times and records clean data — A goes 2 of 3, B goes 3 of 3. Then he chooses A because of its single best trial, the dead-center hit. That is precision, not reliability: he picks the strategy that hit hardest once over the one that hit every time. The whole sheet is built to separate those two ideas. Prompt: "Which strategy would you trust on a day you only get one shot? Compare the hit rates, not the best hit."
