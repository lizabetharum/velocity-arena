Key idea. Charge speed grows by the square (quadratic). Follow-through grows in a straight line (linear). The same +1 to Power does not add the same amount each time.

Quadratic, so the steps aren't equal. Going 1→2 adds only 3% charge (1→4). Going 7→8 adds 15% (49→64). The same one point of Power is worth 5× more at the top of the curve than the bottom. So Power is all-or-nothing: either commit enough points to reach the high-value zone, or spend them on a stat that pays evenly like Speed. Half-measures in Power waste budget.

Teacher look-for
Priya analyzes marginal gain: +3% charge at the bottom of the curve, +15% at the top, a 5× difference for the same one point. She turns that into an allocation rule — commit to Power or skip it, because middle values waste budget. This is calculus-adjacent reasoning (rate of change along a curve) surfaced in plain language, and it directly informs strategy.
