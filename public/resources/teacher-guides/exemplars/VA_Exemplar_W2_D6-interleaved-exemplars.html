Concept Tag Reference
unit rateA fixed amount of output per 1 unit of input
linear decayOutput that decreases at a constant rate over time
linear functionOutput that increases at a constant rate with a non-zero starting value
nonlinear functionOutput that grows by a changing amount as input increases
proportional scalingOutput that scales in direct proportion to input from zero
ratioA part-to-whole relationship within a fixed total

Answer: 30 %

Answer: 46

Answer: 0.32

Answer: 16 %

Teacher Look-For — Marty T. (Approaching)
Marty gets every numerical answer right — he can square numbers and apply all formulas correctly. His one coherent error is tagging the Charge Speed formula (P²) as "proportional scaling" in problems 4, 11, and the Extra Credit, when the correct tag is "nonlinear function." His reasoning is visible: Power goes in, percentage comes out — to him that looks proportional. He's not recognizing that P=4 giving 16% and P=8 giving 64% isn't a 2× relationship — it's 4×. That non-constant ratio is exactly what makes it nonlinear, not proportional. Ask: "If P doubles from 4 to 8, does the charge percentage double? What does that tell you about whether it's proportional?"
