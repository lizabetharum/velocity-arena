bY-interceptYour starting win probability when your stat is zero → y − (m × x)

Step 2  ·  Find the Slope (M)
1—
m = (y₂ − y₁) ÷ (x₂ − x₁)
Formula
2—
m = (6 − 4) ÷ (56 − 52)
Plug In
3—
m = 2 ÷ 4
Subtract
4—
m = 0.5
Divide
Step 3  ·  Find the Y-Intercept (B)
1—
b = y − (m × x)
Formula
2—
b = 52 − (0.5 × 4)
Plug In
3—
b = 52 − 2
Multiply
4—
b = 50
Subtract
Double-check: If m = 0, both matches had the same stat value — pick different matches. If your m is bigger than 10 or smaller than −10, you may have x and y switched.

where x = your Speed stat

Sample — pre-pilot Marcus T.  ·  Team Voltage  ·  Week 3, Day 13
Teacher Look-For — Approaching
Marcus correctly wrote the slope formula and did both subtractions accurately (Δy = 4, Δx = 2) — but he put the x-difference in the numerator and the y-difference in the denominator, giving m = 0.5 instead of the correct m = 2. This is his recurring pattern: individual pieces computed right, the operation that binds them inverted. The consequence shows up clearly in his residuals, which grow larger as x increases — exactly the signature of a slope that is 4× too small. He even notices the pattern ("getting worse and worse") without pinpointing the cause. Ask: "The formula says (y₂ − y₁) on top and (x₂ − x₁) on the bottom — which difference did you put in the numerator when you plugged in?"
