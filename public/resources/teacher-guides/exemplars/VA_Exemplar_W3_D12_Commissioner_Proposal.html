Commissioner Proposal — propose a rule, prove it with data

Students propose a league rule (a cap or floor on a stat), back it with formula-output evidence, and compute the percent change it forces on the last-place team. The low sample divides by the new value instead of the old. The high sample carries the percent change through the quadratic and uses it to prove the rule is fair. Illustrative samples, built before the pilot.

Teacher look-for
The rule is reasonable and his evidence is real — he even runs Motor Power = Speed × 5 correctly (5 × 5 = 25%). The break is the percent change: he divides by the new value (÷ 5) and gets 40% instead of dividing by the old value (÷ 3) for the correct 66.7%. His fairness statement asserts fairness without analyzing whether the rule helps him. Prompt: "Percent change measures growth from where you started. Which number is the starting point, 3 or 5?"

Rule type: CAP (≤) FLOOR (≥)
2 · Data Evidence · Motor Power = Speed × 5 · Charge Speed = Power²
Data Point 1
Team: Sparkz · Power = 1 · Charge = 1² = 1%. Under my rule Power = 3 → 3² = 9%. The floor helps weak teams 9×, not 3×, because charge is quadratic.
Data Point 2
Team: Overclock (us) · Power = 4 → 4² = 16%, unchanged. The rule does not touch my team.
3 · Effect on the Last-Place Team
Last-place team: Sparkz · value now: 1 · under my rule: Goes UP · new value: 3
% change = ( new − old ) ÷ old × 100 = stat: ( 3 − 1 ) ÷ 1 × 100 = 200 % · charge output: ( 9 − 1 ) ÷ 1 × 100 = 800 %
A 200% change in the Power stat becomes an 800% change in charge output, because charge is Power squared. The floor helps the weakest team most — which is the point of a floor.
4 · Fairness Statement
Does this rule also benefit your own team? YesNo
No. My Power is already 4, so the floor changes nothing for me. I proposed a rule that helps my rivals and not myself — that is what makes it fair, and I can prove it with the numbers.
