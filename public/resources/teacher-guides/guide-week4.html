Week 4 at a Glance

Week 4 is the culmination of everything. Season 2 runs Days 13–15. Three matches determine the final standings — the team with the most wins is the Season 2 champion. Three new math concepts land in Week 4: Win Equation (Day 13), residuals (Day 13), and percent change (Day 14). The post-diagnostic runs on Day 15 alongside the final Season 2 match. Day 16 is the Community Exhibition and Awards only.

The most important thing you do in Week 4: Keep the learning visible during the celebration. Day 16 is high energy. Students will want to replay the season and focus on who won. Your job is to make sure the math stays present, the Exhibition requires formula explanations, awards are anchored to data, and the champion is identified by the standings.
Day by Day — Week 4
Day 13 · Week 4
Win Equation + Prediction Market + Season 2 Match 1 + Residuals
Season 2 Begins
Session Flow
  • 20 minHook: The Prediction Market. Each team writes a Season 2 win total prediction on an index card: 'We will win [X] of [Y] matches.' Cards post publicly; every team's prediction is visible.
  • 50 minChallenge Block: Building and Solving Linear Equations. Compute residual = predicted − actual for each Season 1 match. Interpret: positive residual means over-prediction. Negative means under-prediction. What patterns emerge?
  • 20-60 minSnack/Lunch Break
  • 90 minMatch-Build-Make: Season 2 Match Day 1. First matches under the new Commissioner-approved rules. Before each match, teams log their equation-based prediction. After each match, actual result is recorded. Loss-recovery runs for every losing team. Teams compare today's residuals to Season 1 residuals. Ball modes are new. Round 1 is Standard (foam ball). Round 2 is Chaos (ping pong — rewards Turning). Round 3 is Heavy Touch (rewards Power). Before Rounds 2 and 3, say the one-sentence ball mode framing below. Without it, students will blame their slope when the ball mode changed the stat–winning relationship.
  • 35 minOpen Lab: Teams compare their two residuals and wins-per-Speed-point unit rate to Season 2 match prediction and actual. Skills Arena Open.
Prediction Market — Day 13 Setup
  • Each team publicly posts their predicted Season 2 win total before the first match of Day 13
  • Predictions are posted on a visible board — everyone can see everyone else's predictions
  • After each match, teams compute the residual between their predicted running total and actual wins
  • The team with the smallest cumulative residual across all Season 2 matches (Days 13–15) wins the Prediction Market award
  • Teams can observe others' predictions and use them analytically — not to trash talk, but to formulate match strategy
Math Focus
residual = predicted − actual
Residuals connect directly to the gap sentence. The gap sentence was informal residual analysis all along — now students see the formal term. Positive residual = model over-predicted (bot underperformed the model). Negative residual = model under-predicted (bot exceeded the model).
Key Questions to Ask
  • "What is a residual — in your own words, with an example from your Season 1 log?"
  • "Your residual is +2. Did your bot over-perform or under-perform the model?"
  • "Which match had the largest absolute residual? What decision caused that gap?"
  • "How does your Win Equation residual pattern inform your Season 2 allocation?"
Day 14 · Week 4
Percent Change + Season 2 Match Day 2 + Stat Auction
Percent Change + Chaos Mode
Session Flow
  • 30 minHook: Stat Auction + Prediction Check. Twenty-five minutes of competitive stat bidding, then a 5-minute prediction check. Each team starts with 3 math tokens; they earn more by answering formula questions correctly. Teams bid on stat improvements but must calculate the new formula output out loud to win. Wrong math costs tokens. After the auction, teams revisit their Season 2 prediction on the wall and update it with data justification if needed.
  • 50 minChallenge Block: Percent Change: Season 1 to Season 2 Configurations. Apply to stat values: how much did teams change each stat from Season 1 to Season 2? Which stat changes produced the largest performance change?
  • 20-60 minSnack/Lunch Break
  • 90 minMatch-Build-Make: Season 2 Match Day 2. Ball mode: Chaos (ping pong balls). Stat Auction runs before the match — teams bid on temporary stat adjustments using earned points from Prediction Market.
  • 30 minOpen Lab: Teams look at residuals from the last two match days; if model error has increased, identify one variable to add. Teams test new stat points from the Stat Auction under match conditions and check whether performance matches formula predictions. Skills Arena badge challenges available.

Stat Auction — Rules

Teams bid Prediction Market points to temporarily adjust one stat for one match. Highest bidder gets the adjustment. The auction creates a real economic constraint — spend points on a stat boost or save them for the Prediction Market award. Every bid requires a formula justification.

Math Focus
% change = (new − old) ÷ old × 100
Apply to allocation changes (S went from 8 to 10: what percent increase?), to performance changes (wins went from 1 to 3: what percent increase?), and to stat efficiency (Motor% change per unit of Speed change).
Ball Mode: Chaos
Chaos mode uses ping pong balls. Lighter, faster, less predictable than the standard tennis ball. Power becomes more influential — Follow-Through time matters more than Charge% because the ball moves quickly once contacted. Teams should consider whether their Power allocation is optimized for Chaos mode before the match.
Key Questions
  • ? "Your Speed went from 6 to 10. What percent increase is that? What's the Motor% change?"
  • ? "Your wins went from 0 in Season 1 to 2 in Season 2 so far. What percent change?"
  • ? "In Chaos mode — does your allocation favor the right stats for ping pong balls? Why?"

    Math Focus
    Bot Identity = All Concepts Applied to One Bot
    The Bot Identity card is a synthesis task. Every formula, every concept, every data point from 15 days applied to one specific bot. Students who can complete a full Bot Identity card have achieved transfer — not just recall.
    Ball Mode: Heavy Touch
    Heavy Touch mode. Heavier, slower, requires more deliberate contact force. Charge% becomes the dominant Power metric because you need maximum strike force. Teams should verify their P² Charge% is sufficient for the foam ball before the match.

    The Season 2 champion is determined by win record from Days 13–15. Day 16 is Exhibition and Awards only. Post-diagnostic was completed on Day 15.
    Exhibition Format
    What Students Must Explain
    • Their bot's name and allocation (all 4 stat values)
    • One formula — computed with their actual numbers
    • Their biggest gap sentence from either season (all 3 parts)
    • One thing they changed from Season 1 to Season 2 and why
    Facilitation Role During Exhibition

    Your job is to keep guests asking questions, not to answer them yourself. If a guest looks confused, redirect to the student: "Can you show them the formula on your Bot Identity card?" Students teaching is the point. Stay out of the way.

    Key Questions for Exhibition
    • "Can you explain to our guest what Motor% means and how you calculated yours?"
    • "Show our guest one gap sentence from your Match Log — and explain what it means."
    • "What would you do differently if you were starting over with Budget = 20?"
    Residual Analysis — Quick Reference
    Residual ValueMeaningWhat It SuggestsStudent Action
    Positive (+) Model predicted higher than actual result Bot under-performed relative to allocation — something in the match conditions wasn't captured in the model Check what changed match-day (ball mode, opponent allocation, field position) that formulas don't account for
    Negative (−) Model predicted lower than actual result Bot over-performed relative to allocation — opponent may have had a weaker allocation or there was an opponent error Identify what gave unexpected advantage — can that be replicated or built into future allocation?
    Zero (0) Model exactly matched actual result High model accuracy for this match — formulas are working as expected for the given allocation Note which allocation + opponent combination produces zero residuals — that's your strongest matchup profile
    Large |residual| Large gap between prediction and result Model may be missing an important variable — or there was a mechanical issue or coding error that changed performance Flag for root-cause analysis. Don't revise the allocation based on one large residual — look for a pattern across matches.

    Week 4 Critical Risks

    Post-Diagnostic Creep to Day 16

    If the post-diagnostic gets delayed to Day 16, it competes with Exhibition energy and the awards ceremony. Students will rush through it or disengage entirely. Run it on Day 15 alongside the final match. No exceptions.

    Exhibition Becomes Performance, Not Explanation

    Day 16 Exhibition can drift toward "show off the bot" instead of "teach the math." If teams are demoing without explaining formulas, redirect: "Show our guest the formula you used and what number it produced." The explanation is the point, not the demo.

    Residual Confusion

    Students often confuse residual sign. "Positive means good" is a common error — but a positive residual means the model over-predicted, which means the bot under-performed expectations. Anchor this with concrete examples from their own Match Logs.

    Exhibition Facilitator Over-Presence

    When guests ask questions during the Exhibition and the facilitator jumps in to answer, it undercuts the student as the expert. Your role is redirector, not explainer. If it goes quiet, ask the student: "Can you show them in your Match Log?"
