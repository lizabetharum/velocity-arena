Match Catch-Up Plan

The final 5 sessions. The activities stalled because there was no match data to work with — so this plan generates the data first, then runs the analysis that was blocked. Send Liz the data (recorded on paper) and she will backfill the 2 matches already played, then run two match blocks (Season 1 on Session 1, Season 2 on Session 3) — you need matches on both sides of a stat change to compare. The 5 sessions run Tue Aug 11, Wed 12, Thu 13, Fri 14, and Mon 17. Each session runs 9:00–2:00 with an hour for lunch — about 4 hours of programming a day; the clock times below fill that window. Have ready daily: robots, devices, the paper match log.

This replaces Days 10–16 of the original plan. The full 16-day arc it's catching up to: the original 16-day schedule.

Three things that must happen — do not skip

1. Backfill the 2 matches correctly (Session 1, morning). In the dashboard Match Log, each row needs the right Week (Season 1 = Week 2), a Prediction source and Predicted goals (Goals For / Goals Against stay locked until you pick a source), the actual GF/GA, and a one-sentence "what happened." Wrong week = the row doesn't count. How to log a match · full workflow.

2. Session 4: every student takes the Post-Task and the Reflection online — the pilot's measurement against Day 1. Names exactly as Day 1. Do it even if you run out of time for everything else.

3. Session 5: students teach their own math to a visitor. Don't answer for them.

The season rule — keep it straight

Season 1 (Sessions 1–2): teams keep the loadout they already have — no stat changes. Reallocating stats starts in Season 2 (Session 3). Don't let a team re-tune stats before then, or the Season 1 → Season 2 comparison falls apart.

If a match block runs short or a team has no opponent

You don't need a full round-robin — 2–3 rounds per block is enough data to see a trend. If teams are idle, a solo Skills Arena drill or a Distance-Lab re-run gives the same predict-vs-actual data to log. Skills Arena.
