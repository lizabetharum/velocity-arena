Match Catch-Up Plan

The final 7 sessions. The activities stalled because there was no match data to work with — so this plan generates the data first, then runs the analysis that was blocked. Send Liz the data (recorded on paper) and she will backfill the 2 matches already played, then run two short match blocks (one this week in Season 1, one next week in Season 2) instead of cramming matches into one day — you need matches on both sides of a stat change to compare. 3 sessions this week, field trip Friday, 4 next week. Each session runs 10:00–3:00 with an hour for lunch — about 4 hours of programming a day; the clock times below fill that window. Have ready daily: robots, devices, the paper match log.

This replaces Days 10–16 of the original plan. The full 16-day arc it's catching up to: the original 16-day schedule.

Three things that must happen — do not skip

1. Backfill the 2 matches correctly (Session 1, morning). In the dashboard Match Log, each row needs the right Week (Season 1 = Week 2), a Prediction source and Predicted goals (Goals For / Goals Against stay locked until you pick a source), the actual GF/GA, and a one-sentence "what happened." Wrong week = the row doesn't count. How to log a match · full workflow.

2. Session 6: every student takes the Post-Task and the Reflection online — the pilot's measurement against Day 1. Names exactly as Day 1. Do it even if you run out of time for everything else.

3. Session 7: students teach their own math to a visitor. Don't answer for them.

The season rule — keep it straight

Season 1 (through Session 2): teams change only their predictions — stats stay locked. Reallocating stats starts in Season 2 (Session 3). Don't let a team re-tune stats before then, or the Season 1 → Season 2 comparison falls apart.

Residuals + percent change Session 2 · recovers Day 8–10

  1. 10:00Residuals + prediction-market review. For each round, gap = predicted − actual. Is the gap shrinking round to round? Read the market in miss-to-hit order; name the best prediction.
  2. 11:00Percent change on your accuracy. How much did your prediction error shrink? (first gap − latest gap) ÷ first gap × 100. Put a number on how much better your model got.Lesson: Percent Change (EN | ES toggle).
  3. 12:00Lunch (1 hour).
  4. 1:00Name the cause of your residual pattern — slope error, x-variable choice, model drift, or environmental sensitivity — in one sentence, citing a round.
  5. 1:40Compare with another team. Read their residuals; write one thing to keep and one to re-check in your own model, citing a number.
  6. 2:20Lock the skill. Work the interactive Percent Change practice; early finishers head to Skills Arena.Percent Change · Skills Arena.

Ends: every team has residuals, a percent-change-in-accuracy number, and a named cause.

If a match block runs short or a team has no opponent

You don't need a full round-robin — 2–3 rounds per block is enough data to see a trend. If teams are idle, a solo Skills Arena drill or a Distance-Lab re-run gives the same predict-vs-actual data to log. Skills Arena. If matches fall through entirely, switch to the No-Match Backup Plan.
