Launch Sequence — Answer Key

Every team's numbers are different — different ball position, different bot. This gives you one fully worked bot on the left and, for each step, the rule for checking any team on the right. The worked bot: equation distance = 38 × time − 7; the teacher placed the ball 60 cm across, 80 cm up.

Set the ball on a Pythagorean triple if you want clean numbers. 30–40 (→50), 60–80 (→100), 90–120 (→150), or 50–120 (→130) all give a whole-number diagonal. It removes calculator fuss and lets you see instantly whether a team's diagonal is right.

1–2 Measure, then the distance formula

Worked bot · 60 across, 80 up

diagonal = √(across² + up²)

= √(60² + 80²) = √(3600 + 6400) = √10000 = 100 cm.

Check any team

Square each side, add, square-root. Watch two slips: squaring wrong (60² is 3600, not 120), and adding before rooting — √(a²+b²) is not a + b.

If you gave a triple, the diagonal should be the whole number (e.g. 100). If it isn't, their arithmetic is off.

3 Diagonal → drive time

Worked bot

time = (100 − (−7)) ÷ 38 = 107 ÷ 38 = 2.816 s.

× 1000 → driveMs = 2816.

Check any team

Their diagonal into their own equation: (diagonal + their start-up-flipped) ÷ their speed, then × 1000. The sign trap is the same as the Target Challenge — start-up is negative, so it adds.

4–5 Aim, launch, tune

Two different misses

Short / long → the math (drive time). Re-check the diagonal and the ÷ speed.

Left / right → the aim, not the math. Re-point the bot; the diagonal is still right.

The key coaching move

Separate the two errors. A team that drove the right distance but aimed off should NOT change their numbers — only their aim. A team that aimed true but stopped short/long fixes the math. Mixing the two is the main way teams go in circles.

The one thing to test before the competition: does a bot, aimed and driven to the ball, actually kick it into the goal? Do a couple of dry launches yourself. Wide goal + short distance behind the ball makes this reliable. If the kick is flaky, widen the goal or move it closer — do not let a weak kick get blamed on the math.

6 The competition

It's fair by construction. Everyone launches from the same spot at the same ball, and everyone drives the exact distance from their own equation — so the winner has the sharpest math + aim, not the luckiest setup. Most goals out of 5; one neutral ref calls in/out. Bracket it if you want a champion.

7 Why the diagonal

Worked bot

Two-leg path = 60 + 80 = 140 cm. Diagonal = 100 cm. The diagonal saves 40 cm.

The idea

The straight line between two points is always shorter than going around two sides of the triangle — the triangle inequality, and the reason a good launch cuts the corner. Any team's diagonal should be less than (across + up) but more than the longer single side.
