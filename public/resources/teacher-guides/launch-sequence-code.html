Launch Sequence — the code

Your opening move: turn toward the ball, drive the diagonal in blocks, turn back the same angle to face the goal. Paste it into STUDENT ZONE B of the match code and fill in two numbers — your angle and your block count.

You fill in exactly two numbers: your angle (it appears twice — the turn and the turn-back are the same) and your block count. The locked part of the match code drives the last bit to the ball and kicks (strength = Power²).
  1. Open the match code in MakeCode and find ★ STUDENT ZONE B — LAUNCH SEQUENCE ★ (the // YOUR CODE HERE line).
  2. Work out your angle = tan⁻¹(across ÷ up) — or just 45° if across = up — and your blocks = distance ÷ 27.
  3. Type these three moves in, filling your two numbers into the ★ spots.
  4. Download, press A to set up, press A again to launch. Check the bot ends up behind the ball, facing the goal.
// ═══════════════════════════════════════════════
//   LAUNCH SEQUENCE — turn, drive to the ball, turn back
//   Goes inside ★ STUDENT ZONE B ★ of the match code.
//   Fill in TWO numbers: your angle and your block count.
// ═══════════════════════════════════════════════

// 1. turn to face the ball
CutebotPro.trolleySteering(CutebotProTurn.LeftInPlace, 45)   // ★ YOUR ANGLE ★
basic.pause(300)
CutebotPro.stopImmediately(CutebotProMotors.ALL); basic.pause(150)

// 2. drive the diagonal   (1 block = 27 cm)
CutebotPro.runBlockCnt(4)                            // ★ YOUR BLOCK COUNT ★
basic.pause(150)

// 3. turn back the SAME angle to re-face the goal
CutebotPro.trolleySteering(CutebotProTurn.RightInPlace, 45)  // ★ SAME ANGLE ★
basic.pause(150)
Why blocks? runBlockCnt(n) drives exactly n × 27 cm, the same real distance on every bot — so your geometry holds no matter how fast your bot is. Worked example: ball 81 cm forward and 81 cm across → angle 45°, c ≈ 115 cm → 115 ÷ 27 ≈ 4 blocks.
