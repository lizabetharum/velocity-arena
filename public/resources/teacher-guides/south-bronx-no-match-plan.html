No-Match Backup Plan

The final 7 sessions — no matches. This class couldn't record matches, so the same math runs on the bot alone: the Distance Lab, Target Challenge, and Skills Arena drive the same loop a match would — predict → run → measure the gap → improve → teach. 3 sessions this week, field trip Friday, 4 next week. Fit the minute counts to your daily bell schedule. Have ready daily: robots, student devices, paper/pencils.

Two things that must happen — do not skip

1. Session 6: every student takes the Post-Task and the Reflection online. This is the pilot's whole measurement against Day 1. Open the Post-Task (~12 min, silent), then the Reflection. Each student types first name, last initial, and team exactly as on Day 1, or their results can't be matched. Do it even if you run out of time for everything else.

2. Session 7: students teach their own math to a visitor. Don't answer for them.

This week — 3 sessions

Reset + build your equation Session 1 · Distance Lab

  1. 15mReset the goal. No matches from here — you'll prove your math with the bot on its own. It's the same loop as a match: predict, run, measure the gap, improve.
  2. 90mDistance Lab. Drive 1 second, measure the cm (your speed). Predict a longer drive, run it, measure the miss, fix ONE number. Five trials, then write distance = speed × time + start-up — that's y = mx + b.Guide: Distance Lab. Worksheet: EN · ES. Answer key.
  3. lunchLunch.
  4. 45mEnter your loadout + equation in the dashboard. Pick your site (South Bronx), name your team, and save.Team Data Dashboard.

Ends: every team has a written equation built from its own driving data.

Precision with your equation Session 2 · Target Challenge

  1. 15mWarm-up. Each team restates its equation and what the speed and start-up numbers mean.
  2. 100mTarget Challenge. Tape a target line. Solve the drive time from your equation — time = (target − start-up) ÷ speed, ×1000 for milliseconds — program it, drive. Practice targets, then a surprise target: closest wins.Worksheet: EN · ES. Answer key.
  3. lunchLunch.
  4. 40mLog predicted vs. actual for each shot and find the gap. This is your prediction-vs-result data — the same thing a match would give you.

Ends: every team hits a surprise target and has predicted-vs-actual data.

Percent change + start the poster Session 3

  1. 45mPercent change. How much did fixing your method improve your speed? (new − old) ÷ old × 100. Put a real number on your improvement.Lesson: Percent Change (EN | ES toggle).
  2. 60mStart the equation poster. Your equation and a graph of your five trial dots.How-to: Equation Poster. Printable worksheet: EN · ES.
  3. lunchLunch.
  4. 30mCompare equations with another team. Write one thing to keep and one to re-check in your own — citing a number.

Ends: every team has a percent-change result and a started poster.

Friday — field trip (no session)

No camp session Friday. Pick up at Session 4 on Monday.

Next week — 4 sessions

Change one stat, predict, re-test Session 4 · Season 2, no matches

  1. 20mThe Season 2 move — without matches. Now you may reallocate stats (in Season 1 you only changed predictions). Pick ONE stat to change and predict its effect using proportional scaling: Motor Power = Speed × 5 scales the same amount; Charge = Power² grows much faster.
  2. 90mMake the change, then re-test with a solo run — re-run a Distance Lab drive or a Skills Arena drill and measure the change with percent change. Log the loadout change in the dashboard's Stat Change Log.Dashboard · Percent Change.
  3. lunchLunch.
  4. 40mDid it help? Write one sentence citing your data (not a match): "We changed ___; our ___ went from ___ to ___ — a ___% change."

Ends: every team made one data-driven stat change and measured whether it helped.

Skills Arena + finish the poster Session 5

  1. 90mSkills Arena. Drive straight, turn exact angles, park on a spot — the performance arena that stands in for matches, and great practice for the same driving the Distance Lab measured.Skills Arena (EN | ES toggle, and a light/dark reading toggle) · Answer key.
  2. lunchLunch.
  3. 60mFinish the poster. Equation, graph, percent-change result, and a QR to your shared design. Print in black & white on 8.5 × 11 — the QR opens the color version.Worksheet: EN · ES.

Ends: poster done and driving sharpened.

Teach the Math + Post-Task Session 6

  1. 60mTeach the Math. Explain your equation in plain language with your real numbers — no robot jargon. Teach-back rehearsal: a partner plays a confused visitor; you explain ONCE and let them try — no rescuing. Fix the sentence that didn't land, then switch.Teach the Math (EN | ES toggle).
  2. lunchLunch.
  3. 50mPost-Task Diagnostic + Reflection. 12 minutes, individual, silent — then the Reflection. Names exactly as on Day 1. Protect this; never cut it.Post-Task · Reflection.
  4. 40mSet up your exhibition station. Poster up, bot ready, target line down. Do one full dry run.

Ends: explanation rehearsed; Post-Task + Reflection done.

Community Exhibition + closing Session 7

  1. 30mLast setup + rehearse the one sentence you rewrote in Session 6.
  2. 90mCommunity Exhibition. A visitor solves a problem using your equation — you teach, they solve, you check. Hand each visitor a Visitor Sheet; each student keeps a Teach-a-Visitor tracker. Stay out of it; the students teach.Teach-a-Visitor: EN · ES. Visitor Sheet: EN · ES.
  3. 30mClosing circle + awards by the math — Most Improved (biggest percent change), Best Analyst, Best Teacher — named with the calculation, not match standings. Each student finishes: "On Day 1 I couldn't ___. Now I can ___."

Ends: every student taught a visitor their own math and named their growth.

What you are NOT doing — and that's fine

No matches, brackets, standings, scouting reports, or Commissioner proposals. This class couldn't record matches, and that's okay — the Distance Lab, Target Challenge, and Skills Arena give the same real math (build an equation, test it, improve it, teach it).

Seasons: in Season 1 teams change only their predictions; here in Season 2 they may reallocate stats and re-test — but with solo bot runs, not matches. If a link won't open or something is missing, contact the site coordinator.
