The 5 Stat Formulas
StatDay IntroducedFormulaRangeWhat It Controls
Speed (S) Day 1 Motor% = S ÷ 20 × 100 1–17 Drive motor power percentage
Endurance (E) Day 2 P(t) = 100 − (20−E) × 1.5 × t 1–17 Power remaining after t minutes
Turning (T) Day 3 Ratio = 0.2 + T × 0.02 1–17 Left-to-right motor speed ratio
Power (P) Day 4 Charge% = P²  Follow-Through = P × 80 1–10 Ball strike force and push distance
Budget (B) Day 5 S + P + E + T = 20 Fixed = 20 All stats must sum to exactly 20 (Season 1). Last-place team gets 22 in Season 2.

Note: Power range is 1–10 because P² values grow quickly. Budget = 22 for the last-place team in Season 2 only — the underdog mechanic, revealed Day 11.

Team Roles — Rotate Weekly
💻
Coder
Translates stat allocations into micro:bit code. Owns all programming changes. Responsible for match-day deployment.
🔧
Fabricator
Maintains bot hardware, scoop mechanics, and physical build quality. First responder for mechanical issues.
📊
Analyst
Owns the Match Log, gap sentences, and Dashboard updates. Presents data during debrief. Writes all gap sentences.

Roles rotate every week (Days 1, 5, 9, 13). Every student holds every role at least once across 4 weeks.

  • Day 2
    Bot Naming CeremonyTeams name their bot and post it permanently on the wall. That name anchors team identity for all 4 weeks.
  • End of Day 5
    Allocations LockedTeams submit final stat allocations (summing to 20). Numbers are frozen until Season 2 reallocation.
  • Day 10
    Season 1 FinalSeason 1 concludes with standings.
  • Day 12
    Commissioner's Meeting + VoteTeams present proposals using data. Class votes on Season 2 rule changes. This is the highest-risk day for over-scaffolding.
  • Day 16

Three Highest-Risk Moments
Risk 1
Diagnostic Shame
Days 1 + 15
Students who freeze on the pre-diagnostic or tank the post-diagnostic need identity protection, not remediation talk. Frame both as "what the bot needs to know" — not what the student doesn't know.
Risk 2
First Loss Spiral
Match days 7–10, 13–15
Loss triggers identity threat for some students. The 3-step loss-recovery protocol must be used exactly: (1) acknowledge emotion 30 sec (2) locate one decision in data (3) restore hypothesis-maker role. Do not skip steps or add steps.
Risk 3
Commissioner's Meeting Over-Scaffolding
Day 12
The urge to "help" teams fix weak proposals destroys the authentic tension the lesson depends on. Weak proposals go to the floor. The class decides. Your job is process facilitation, not proposal quality control.
4 Design Principles
01
Every number earns a trade-off
Raising one stat always lowers another. The budget constraint is the engine of all mathematical reasoning in the program. Never let a team escape the trade-off.
02
Data before opinion
Every claim a student makes must be anchored to a number. "I think we're fast" is not acceptable. "Our Speed is 8, which gives Motor% = 40%" is. Hold this standard from Day 1.
03
Losing is information
A loss with a gap sentence is worth more than a win without one. The program teaches students to treat results as data, not verdicts. This reframe is your most important facilitation move.
04
Competition is the container, not the content
Winning matters enough to motivate. It doesn't matter enough to override learning. When a team abandons analysis for "just fix the bot," pull them back to the worksheet.
Non-Negotiables
  • 7-second wait time after every question — no exceptions, no shortcuts
  • Gap sentences require all 3 parts: predicted value / actual result / quantified gap
  • Loss-recovery protocol is 3 steps
  • Allocations locked end of Day 5 — no changes until Season 2 reallocation
  • Budget = 20 for all teams in Season 1. Budget = 22 for last-place team in Season 2 only
  • Bot Naming Ceremony names are permanent for all 4 weeks
  • Commissioner proposals must cite two specific data points
  • Cold Recall Check (Day 10): individual, no bot context, 5 questions, 10 minutes
  • Post-diagnostic on Day 15
  • Every student rotates through every role across 4 weeks
Sentence Starters by Function
Gap Sentence
  • "We predicted ___, but the result was ___, so our gap is ___."
  • "Our formula gave ___, the actual outcome was ___, a difference of ___."
Pressing for Evidence
  • "Where in your data does it show that?"
  • "What number are you using to support that?"
  • "Which formula connects to that decision?"
Loss Recovery
  • "That result was frustrating. Name the feeling — 30 seconds."
  • "Now find one decision in your log. Just one."
  • "What would you test differently? You're still the scientist here."
Commissioner Proposal
  • "My proposal is ___, supported by [data point 1] and [data point 2]."
  • "This change affects the last-place team by ___."
  • "The concept this involves is ___."
Redirecting Opinion
  • "I hear a preference — what does your data say?"
  • "That's a hypothesis. How would you test it?"
  • "What formula would help you check that?"
ENDS Closing Ritual
  • "One number you're taking out of today is ___."
  • "The math move we used today was ___ because ___."
  • "Before next time, I want to test whether ___."
