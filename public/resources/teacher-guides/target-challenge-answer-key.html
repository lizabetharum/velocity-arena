Target Challenge — Answer Key

There is no single set of right numbers. Every team's equation is different, so every team's times are different — and different is correct. This key gives you one fully worked bot on the left and, for each step, the rule that decides whether any team's work is right on the right. Grade against the rule, not the numbers.

Do not project this. Read it yourself and go ask questions at the tables.
The one idea to hold onto: the bot's speed is fixed — it's the cm-per-second from the Distance Lab equation. What students solve for and set is the drive TIME. Different targets → different times → same speed. Watch for teams who try to change their speed instead; the speed does not change.
The worked bot: equation distance = 38 × time − 7, so speed = 38 cm/s and start-up = −7 cm. Use it to sanity-check the shape of a team's work; do not expect any team to match these numbers.

1 Copy your equation

Worked bot

distance = 38 × time − 7.

Speed = 38 cm/s  ·  start-up = −7 cm.

Check any team

Speed = the number in front of time (the slope). Start-up = the second number, and it should be negative.

"Speed" here is the cm-per-second from the equation — not their Speed stat. If a team wrote their Speed stat (a small number like 5), that's the wrong number.

2 Turn the equation around

Worked bot

time = (target − start-up) ÷ speed

= (target − (−7)) ÷ 38 = (target + 7) ÷ 38.

Check any team

They must subtract the start-up first, then divide by speed — in that order (undo the +start-up, then undo the ×speed).

The sign is the trap. Start-up is negative, so subtracting it adds: target − (−7) = target + 7. A team that writes target − 7 dropped the double negative.

3 Round 1 — aim and fire

Worked bot · target 145 cm

time = (145 + 7) ÷ 38 = 152 ÷ 38 = 4.0 s.

Set 4.0 s, drive. Model says 38×4 − 7 = 145, so the bot lands right on the line. Miss ≈ 0.

Check any team

Their time = (their target + their start-up-flipped) ÷ their speed. Plug their numbers; it should match.

Miss = target − where it actually stopped, as a plain +/− number. A few cm off is a good result.

The 0.5-second catch — expect a small "rounding miss." Button B only sets the time in half-second steps, so most computed times can't be set exactly. Example, target 150: time = (150+7)/38 = 4.13 s → the bot can only do 4.0 s → model predicts 145 → so even a perfect team is about 5 cm short. That miss is the rounding, not a mistake. Tell students to set the nearest half-second and read the real miss off the floor.
Want clean hits? Pick targets a bot can actually reach. For this bot the settable distances are the ones that land on a half-second: 31, 50, 69, 88, 107, 126, 145, 164, 183 cm (that's 1.0, 1.5, 2.0 … 5.0 s). Choosing targets from a team's own list removes the rounding miss and isolates the real model error. Each team's list is different — it's just their equation at 1.0, 1.5, 2.0 … seconds.

4 Change one thing, then aim again

Worked bot

If the bot overshoots every target, the real speed is a little higher than 38 (or the start-up is off). Nudge one number and re-aim.

Check any team

The "one thing" must be named: a better speed, a better start-up, or a straighter drive. "We changed it" isn't an answer.

Across the rounds the miss should shrink (setting aside the half-second rounding). If it doesn't, ask what number they changed and why.

5 The real test — a target they've never aimed at

Worked bot · surprise target 107 cm

time = (107 + 7) ÷ 38 = 114 ÷ 38 = 3.0 s exactly. Model predicts 38×3 − 7 = 107 → an exact hit.

Check any team

Same formula, a target they did not practise. A small miss on a fresh target is the strongest evidence their equation works.

If you called a target off their settable list, expect near-zero. If not, expect the half-second rounding miss and nothing more.

6 Get ready to teach it

  • Their opening sentence should get a visitor to: take the target, add the start-up back (or "add 7"), then divide by the speed. Any plain-language version of that is right.
  • The teach-back check: did the teammate hit the target after one explanation? If they got stuck, the team should have rewritten the exact sentence that failed — look for that rewrite on the sheet.
