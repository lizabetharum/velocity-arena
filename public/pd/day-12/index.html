03
Lobby the Commissioners · 25 min

Pitch the room.

Ten minutes of informal conversation. No presentations. Just walk over.

The pitch

"Here's our proposal. Here's the number behind it. What do you think?"

Then fifteen minutes at your seat to revise based on what you heard.

04
Final Proposal Verification · 35 min

Four jobs. Do them all.

  1. Confirm both cited numbers are real. Open the dashboard. Find each row.
  2. Calculate how much your rule helps the last-place team. Show the percent change.
  3. Confirm it doesn't help your own team more than the last-place team.
  4. Name the math concept behind it: unit rate, ratio, slope, linear decay, proportional scaling.

05
New words for what we're about to do

Two ways data can connect.

Causal

X produces Y, even after you account for everything else. "Higher Speed causes faster motor power" — the formula guarantees it.

Correlational

X and Y happen together — but a third thing might be driving both. "Teams with high Power score more goals" — but maybe high-Power teams also have high Speed.

Crosstown 20 min  ·  NYC 60 min

Break.

Eat, rest, move. Talk about anything except math.

07
Commissioner's Meeting · 90 min

The meeting opens.

Every team has 3 minutes. Cite two data points. Explain your reasoning. Describe how the change affects the last-place team.

Then the league votes.

08
Each team · 3 min on the clock

What goes in three minutes.

  1. Your rule, stated once, clearly. (One sentence.)
  2. Two specific numbers from the dashboard. Show the math you did.
  3. The last-place effect. Name how it changes for them, with a percent.

When the timer hits zero, you stop.

09
After every presentation

"Can anyone verify the math?"

While a team presents, you write down the two numbers they cited and check the percent-change math yourself.

This makes the whole room responsible for what gets voted in.

10
The vote

How it works.

  1. The proposing team does not vote on its own rule.
  2. A rule passes on a majority of the other teams.
  3. If it passes, there's a 2-minute objection window — but the objection only counts if it names a number.
11
If your rule passes

It goes on the wall.
In permanent marker.

Exactly as worded. Visible to every team. Permanent for the rest of the program.

12
Once all proposals are done

Revise your Season 2 allocation.

It has to satisfy every rule that passed — at the same time — and still sum to 20.

(Or 22 if you're the Underdog team.)

14
The exit ticket

Write your hardest constraint as an inequality.

Example

If the rule says "no team's Power can exceed 6," write: P ≤ 6.

Then confirm: "My final allocation satisfies every rule, and the four stats sum to 20 (or 22)."

Closing reflection · 4 min

ENDS ritual.

Name someone else's math

Name one math concept from today's meeting that was in someone else's proposal — not yours. Write the calculation they used. Explain to a teammate why citing that specific number made the argument stronger.
